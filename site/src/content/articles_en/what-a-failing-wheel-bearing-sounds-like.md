A wheel bearing almost never fails suddenly. It travels a long road from a
barely audible hum on the motorway to a growl no music can cover, and it
sounds different at every stage. If you know the sequence you can work out
fairly precisely where you are now — and how much time is left before
putting it off stops being an option.

## Stage one: a hum you only hear on the motorway

It starts as a quiet, even hum, like winter tyres on dry tarmac. It
appears from around 50–60 km/h, grows with speed and does not follow
engine revs. At this point people usually blame the tyres — and that is
the main trap: a bearing and a tyre sound similar but react to the road
differently.

Separating them is simple. Drive over sections with different surfaces:
noisy tyres change their voice with the tarmac, a bearing hums the same
everywhere. Then ease smoothly left and right at 60–80 km/h. In a curve
the weight shifts outwards and the loaded worn bearing hums louder. If the
hum grows in a left-hand curve, the right side is the suspect, and vice
versa. The full comparison is in
[humming: wheel bearing or tyres](/en/articles/hum-wheel-bearing-or-tyres/).

## Stage two: the hum grows teeth

Next, structure appears in the sound. The even “ooooo” turns into a
pulsing hum with an overtone, as though something were rolling round and
round. The reason is clear: the raceways have started to spall, the balls
or rollers pass over damaged patches, and every pass is audible. The hum
is now noticeable in town too, at 40–50 km/h, not only on the motorway.

This is also usually where the coasting reaction first shows: if you coast
in neutral the sound does not disappear, because the source rotates with
the wheels rather than with the engine. That is an important difference
from transmission noise.

At this stage the bearing is also confidently identified on a raised
wheel: spin it by hand and hold a screwdriver or a pry bar against the
strut, and you hear a distinct rough rustle that the healthy side does not
have. The procedure is set out in
[checking hub play with the wheel raised](/en/articles/checking-wheel-bearing-play-with-the-wheel-raised/).
You can still drive like this for a while, but it is the last calm stage —
after it wear accelerates, because the ruined raceways break up whatever
is left of them.

## Stage three: rumble, vibration, play

| Sign | What it means |
|---|---|
| A growl instead of an even hum | The cage is breaking up, the rolling elements run unevenly |
| Vibration in the wheel and floor | Noticeable radial play has appeared |
| Wheel play when raised | The clearance has grown to a mechanically obvious size |
| A hot hub after a drive | The grease has degraded, friction is rising |
| The hum barely follows the manoeuvre | Wear is so far gone it is audible in any attitude |

Any of the last three means the observation period is over. Beyond it lies
the risk of the bearing seizing or the hub failing on the move, and that
is no longer about comfort but about safety.

## What gets mistaken for a bearing

Not every hum at the front is a hub. Knocking and rattling over bumps far
more often comes from the anti-roll bar links. Vague behaviour in ruts,
wandering and dull thumps are the typical picture of worn control arm
bushes. To compare your case with the standard sound portrait, see the
symptom page [wheel bearing hum](/en/symptoms/wheel-bearing-hum/), and for
the full set of self-checks,
[how to check a wheel bearing yourself](/en/articles/how-to-check-a-wheel-bearing-yourself/).

## How the repair is decided

Two things govern the job. First, whether your car takes a bearing pressed
into the existing hub or a complete hub assembly replaced as a unit: that
decides the time, the tooling and often whether the ABS sensor comes with
it. Second, the hub nut torque — over- or under-tightening kills a new
bearing within a few thousand kilometres, so a torque wrench rather than
an impact gun is what you want to see being used.

There is no need to replace bearings in pairs, but it is worth asking for
the other side to be checked on the lift while the car is up: if it is
also near the end you can plan the work instead of meeting it in a month.
And if the hum came with vibration, ask for the wheel to be balanced
afterwards — a failing bearing often disguises an unbalanced wheel, and
the vibration that remains after the repair surprises people.

If the hum is still quiet and you are not sure you are not imagining it,
