The neutral throttle test is the simplest of diagnostic procedures and at
the same time one of the most useful. The car stands still, the gearbox is
in neutral, you raise the revs smoothly and listen to what changes. The
point is that in this state only the engine and everything it drives are
working: the wheels, the suspension and the transmission are out of the
equation. One movement of the pedal halves the set of possible causes.

## How to run it

The sequence is short, but every step matters:

1. Warm the engine to operating temperature — on a cold engine there are
   too many "normal" sounds.
2. Put the car on the handbrake, the gearbox in neutral or park, and chock
   the wheels.
3. Open the bonnet and stand to one side, by a wing. A helper works the
   pedal.
4. Raise the revs smoothly to 2000–2500, hold for two seconds and return
   just as smoothly. Repeat three or four times.
5. Separately, do one sharp lift-off from 3000 — the moment of lift-off is
   informative in itself.
6. Repeat the cycle with the air conditioning on: some sounds only appear
   under the compressor's load.

What you listen for is behaviour, not loudness. The key questions: does
the sound rise with the revs or live its own life? Does its pitch change
or only its volume? Does it appear on the lift-off or on the way up?

## What the result shows

| How the sound behaves | Likely source |
|---|---|
| Squeal rising with the revs | Drive belt, idler, tensioner |
| Hum or whine tied to the revs | Alternator bearing, water pump |
| Rattle at the moment of lift-off | Stretched timing chain, tensioner |
| Ticking that fades as it warms | Hydraulic lifters, valve clearances |
| Popping in the exhaust on lift-off | Mixture, ignition, an air leak |
| Nothing changes | The source is not connected with the engine |

The last row is the most valuable result of the test. If the sound cannot
be reproduced in neutral at all, there is no point looking under the
bonnet: the cause is in the running gear, the brakes or the tyres. Brake
grinding, for example, only appears with the pedal pressed on the move and
is silent on a stationary car — the signs of that group are covered in
[telling brake pad wear by ear](/en/articles/brake-pad-wear-by-ear/).

## The details that change the picture

A sharp lift-off is a small separate test. At that moment the timing chain
tension falls, the accessories are unloaded, and the pressure in the
intake and exhaust changes. A short metallic rattle on lift-off points at
the timing drive, dull popping in the silencer at the air-fuel mixture and
ignition; the catalogue of that branch is on the symptom page
[exhaust backfire](/en/symptoms/exhaust-backfire/), and the chain case in
[signs of a stretched timing chain](/en/articles/signs-of-a-stretched-timing-chain/).

Switching on the air conditioning engages the compressor — a heavy load on
the belt. A squeal that only appears with the a/c almost always means a
slipping belt or a worn tensioner rather than a failing compressor —
[a squeal: the belt or an idler bearing](/en/articles/squeal-belt-or-idler-bearing/).

One more nuance is where the listener stands. Changing the revs changes
the sound of the whole engine bay at once, and standing in one place makes
it easy to get the source wrong. Walk around the car: from the front, from
the left wing, from the right, and compare.

## What to do with the result

The test does not make a diagnosis, it narrows the search. Afterwards you
have an answer to the main question — engine or not — and a description of
how the sound behaves. That is enough for the conversation at the workshop
to start with specifics rather than "something is making a noise". How to
phrase your observations so a mechanic understands them first time is
covered in
[how to describe a car noise to a mechanic](/en/articles/how-to-describe-a-car-noise-to-a-mechanic/).
If the sound did not reproduce in neutral, the next step is checking the
running gear in the order set out in
[suspension diagnosis by ear: the full checklist](/en/articles/suspension-diagnosis-by-ear-checklist/).

## What the test changes at the workshop

The practical value is in what you no longer pay to have found. A
diagnosis that starts from "it is not the engine" skips the stethoscope
session under the bonnet entirely; one that starts from "the squeal grows
with the a/c on" goes straight to the belt and the tensioner. Both save an
hour of labour, and the observation costs nothing.

Two things worth mentioning to the mechanic afterwards. Whether the sound
followed the revs or the road speed — that single sentence separates the
engine from the running gear more reliably than any adjective. And whether
it appeared on the way up or on the lift-off, because the lift-off is
where the timing drive gives itself away.
