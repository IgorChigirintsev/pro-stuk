"A knock in the engine" is a vague complaint: it covers light ticking,
bright clicking and heavy thumps from deep in the block alike. Yet the
character of the sound is the main clue. It narrows the cause to two or
three versions before the car ever goes on a lift, and the difference
between those versions is fundamental: one fault will calmly wait months,
another finishes the engine off within a hundred kilometres. Here is a map
of the main engine knocks: how each one sounds, in what conditions it
appears and what to do about it.

## A quick table: type of sound and likely cause

The table is a starting point. Find the row that resembles your case; the
details of each group are in the sections below.

| Sound | When it is heard | Likely cause | Urgency |
|---|---|---|---|
| Even ticking | Idle, frequency rises with revs | Valve clearances, hydraulic lifters | Low |
| Clicking, chattering | Idle, more often when warm | Lifters, injectors, timing chain | Low–medium |
| Bright metallic ringing | Acceleration, load, climbs | Detonation | Medium, do not delay |
| A single dull thump | On and off the throttle | Engine mounts | Medium |
| Heavy rapid knock | Under load, rises with revs | Big end bearings | Critical |
| Dull knock when cold | First minutes, then quieter | Piston group, early scoring | Medium |

The conditions matter more than the timbre itself: the same clicking on a
cold and on a hot engine means different things. So while you listen,
record three parameters — engine temperature, revs and load. If the knock
is hard to place in a single row, start by checking the oil level: it is
free and immediately rules out several heavy scenarios. The general
picture for the commonest case is on the symptom page
[knocking in the engine](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-engine/).

## Light sounds: ticking and clicking

High, quiet sounds are usually born in the valve train — the system that
opens and closes the valves. The typical sources:

- **Valve clearances.** On engines with manual adjustment the clearance
  between the cam lobe and the valve drifts from specification over time
  and the valve starts to click —
  [signs of valve clatter](/en/articles/signs-of-valve-clatter/).
- **Hydraulic lifters** — devices that take up the valve clearance using
  oil pressure. They knock when worn, on old oil, or after long standing:
  [how to check hydraulic lifters](/en/articles/how-to-check-hydraulic-lifters/)
  and
  [hydraulic lifters ticking when cold](/en/articles/hydraulic-lifters-ticking-when-cold/).
- **Direct injection injectors** click as standard: it is audible from
  outside on almost any modern direct injection engine and is not a fault.
- **A stretched timing chain** rustles and clicks, most noticeably when
  cold and on a throttle blip —
  [timing chain rustle: what it sounds like](/en/articles/timing-chain-rustle-what-it-sounds-like/).

A detailed breakdown of this group with all the tests is in
[an engine knock at idle](/en/articles/engine-knock-at-idle/), and a quick
self-check on the page
[engine ticking](/en/symptoms/ticking-engine/).

## Bright ringing under load: detonation

If the sound resembles small coins rattling in a tin and appears on
acceleration, uphill or with a loaded car, it is almost always detonation:
the fuel in the cylinder burns as an explosion rather than a smooth front,
and the pressure wave hammers the walls of the combustion chamber. The
classic causes are petrol below the required octane rating, carbon
deposits in the chambers, spark plugs of the wrong heat range, and a
failed knock sensor.

Detonation does not call for a recovery truck, but it must not be
tolerated either: the pressure waves gradually destroy the piston ring
lands. How to tell it from mechanical ringing and what to check first is in
[metallic pinging under acceleration](/en/articles/metallic-pinging-under-acceleration/)
and
[an engine knock under load](/en/articles/engine-knock-under-load/).

## Heavy knocks: bearings, pistons, scoring

A low, rapid knock that rises with revs and grows under load is a sign of
worn bearings: the plain bearings the crankshaft turns in. Big end knock
from the middle of the block is the most dangerous sound in an engine: a
worn shell spins or the rod breaks, and the engine becomes scrap. The same
heavy group includes:

- **piston knock when cold** — the dull muttering of a worn piston skirt
  that fades as the engine warms;
- **knock from bore scoring** — scratches on the cylinder wall; growing
  oil consumption joins the sound, as in
  [signs of cylinder bore scoring](/en/articles/signs-of-cylinder-bore-scoring/);
- **main bearing knock** — lower and less frequent than big end knock,
  felt at the very bottom of the block:
  [signs of main bearing knock](/en/articles/signs-of-main-bearing-knock/).

Telling these apart by ear is hard even for an experienced engine
specialist, so workshops use a stethoscope and cut cylinders one at a
time: when the guilty cylinder is cut, big end knock changes noticeably.
At home it is enough to establish the essentials: the sound is low, rapid,
grows under load and does not go with warm-up — that is sufficient to take
it seriously. The full portrait is in
[signs of big end bearing knock](/en/articles/signs-of-big-end-bearing-knock/).

## How the repair is decided

The gap between the top and the bottom of the table is the gap between a
plug change and a rebuild, and what decides which one you are in is a
handful of cheap measurements rather than opinions: oil pressure on a
mechanical gauge, fault codes and cam timing offset on a scan tool, an
endoscope in the bores, and a cylinder-cut test. Each of them costs a
fraction of the part it rules out, and each is worth asking for by name.

Two things mechanics do not always say. With a heavy knock the car is not
"nursed" to the workshop but transported: a recovery truck is in a
different price league from a spun bearing, and the difference in repair
cost between arriving early and arriving late is severalfold. And after
any bottom-end work the engine needs a gentle running-in and an early
first oil change, with the cause of the oil starvation found — otherwise
the same knock returns on new parts.

## Knocks that do not come from the engine

A noticeable share of "engine knocks" are born next to it. Engine mounts —
the rubber-metal blocks the engine hangs on — give a single dull thump on
and off the throttle when worn:
[signs of worn engine mounts](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-engine-mounts/).
Exhaust heat shields rattle at certain engine speeds, belt idlers and the
tensioner chatter at idle, and a worn alternator decoupler pulley rumbles
after the engine is switched off —
[alternator decoupler pulley failure signs](/en/articles/alternator-decoupler-pulley-failure-signs/).

A quick test to separate them: if the knock is reproduced with the engine
off by rocking the power unit by hand, look at the mountings and the
accessories; if the sound follows the revs strictly, the source is inside
the engine.

Before booking a workshop it helps to answer three questions for yourself:
is the knock heard on a cold engine, a hot one or always; does it depend
on revs or on road speed; does it appear under load or at idle. Those
three answers save the mechanic half the diagnostic time and save you the
cost of it.
