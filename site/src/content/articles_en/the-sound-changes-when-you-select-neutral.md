One of the most useful self-diagnosis techniques looks almost
indecently simple: accelerate to the speed at which the stray sound is
audible and select neutral. How the sound then behaves splits all
possible sources into two large groups — and that saves both time and
money on diagnosis. Here is exactly what the test shows, how to do it
properly and how to read the result.

## What happens in neutral

In neutral the connection between the engine and the wheels is broken.
The engine keeps running at idle, the wheels keep rolling, but torque is
no longer transmitted through the gearbox. So everything that makes noise
because of the drive being transmitted — gears under load, the clutch,
the driveshafts, the power unit mounts — either falls silent or
noticeably changes its voice.

And whatever rotates simply because the car is moving keeps making the
same noise: wheel bearings, tyres, warped brake discs, a shield touching a
disc. Their rotational speed depends only on road speed, and changing gear
means nothing to them.

## How to run the test properly

1. **Choose the place.** A level road with no gradient, no cars nearby,
   50–80 km/h — the speed at which the sound is confidently audible.
2. **Select neutral without switching the engine off.** On an automatic
   that is N; on a manual, press the clutch and take the lever out of
   gear. Hold neutral for a few seconds, listening.
3. **Select the gear again.** On an automatic at a speed appropriate to
   the mode, without a jolt; on a manual with the usual blip.
4. **Repeat two or three times.** One run tells you little: what matters
   is that the change in the sound repeats consistently.

An extra step is to compare coasting under light throttle and without it
in the same gear. If the noise grows specifically under load, that is a
strong separate sign.

## How to read the result

| Behaviour of the sound | Likely group of causes |
|---|---|
| No change at all | Wheel bearing, tyres, brake mechanism |
| Quieter or gone | Gearbox, driveshafts, clutch, mounts |
| Gone along with the vibration | Engine and gearbox mounts, propshaft imbalance |
| Only the pitch changed | Body or exhaust resonance at certain revs |
| A new sound appeared in neutral | Release bearing, gearbox noise at idle |

The last row often surprises: on a manual, a noise heard specifically in
neutral with the clutch up usually belongs to the input shaft and gearbox
bearings rather than the running gear — the separation test is in
[a noisy release bearing: can you drive?](/en/articles/noisy-release-bearing-can-you-drive/).

## What to do with the result

The test does not make a diagnosis, it narrows the field. The logic from
there is simple: if the sound did not change, the next check is raising
the wheels and listening to the hubs; if it did, an inspection of the
mounts and driveshafts on a lift under load. Bear false positives in mind
too: in neutral some of the wind and road noise disappears simply because
you are coasting and usually slowing slightly. So the result counts as
reliable only if it repeats several times.

The difference in cost between "a wheel bearing" and "the gearbox" is
tenfold, which is why five minutes on the test pays for itself. The
individual portraits are in
[signs of a worn wheel bearing](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-wheel-bearing/),
[signs of worn engine mounts](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-engine-mounts/)
and
[gearbox whine at a particular speed](/en/articles/gearbox-whine-at-a-particular-speed/).

Two things worth telling the mechanic afterwards. Whether the sound
followed the road speed or the engine revs, and whether it survived
neutral — those two sentences save an hour of searching and are the whole
point of doing the test at home. And if the answer was "unchanged in
neutral", say so explicitly: it is what keeps an expensive gearbox
investigation off the work order.

If the knock or hum is indistinct and the difference is arguable by ear,
the technique from
[a knock you only hear with the window open](/en/articles/knock-only-audible-with-the-window-open/)
helps, and the full set of road noises is covered on the symptom page
[humming while driving](/en/symptoms/humming-while-driving/).
