A high squeal from under the bonnet almost always leads to one area — the
accessory drive. There are only two candidates there: the drive belt
itself, slipping on the pulleys, and the bearing in one of the idlers or
units the belt runs over. The difference in cost is noticeable, and they
are easy to confuse, because both sounds are high-frequency and both come
from the same place. The good news is that they appear in different
conditions, and by those the diagnosis is made almost without error.

## The belt: a squeal from slipping

The belt squeals when it cannot transmit the effort and slips on a pulley.
So the sound is tied not to engine speed as such but to the moment of
load. Hence its typical portrait:

- a sharp squeal in the first seconds after starting, especially in frost;
- a squeal the moment the air conditioning engages — the compressor loads
  the drive abruptly;
- a squeal on a sharp throttle opening and with the headlights and heaters
  on, when the alternator draws maximum current;
- worse in damp weather and after a wash.

Between those episodes the belt is silent. There are three causes of
slipping: worn grooves and cracks in the belt itself, a sagged tensioner,
and fluids reaching the working face. The sound portrait of start-up
squeals is on the symptom page
[squeal on startup](/en/symptoms/squeal-on-startup/), with the detailed
case in
[a squeal under the bonnet at start-up](/en/articles/squeal-under-the-bonnet-at-startup/).

## The bearing: a whine tied to revs

A bearing behaves in the opposite way: it is indifferent to the load on
the drive but follows engine speed strictly. The sound is even and
continuous, audible at idle and on the move, rising smoothly as the revs
come up and falling the same way. Switching on the air conditioning or the
headlights does not affect it —
[signs of a worn idler pulley](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-idler-pulley/).

| Sign | Belt | Idler bearing |
|---|---|---|
| With the air conditioning on | Grows or appears | Unchanged |
| In the first seconds of start-up | Sharp squeal, then silence | There from the first second and stays |
| In damp weather | Noticeably louder | No change |
| At steady idle | Usually silent | Even whine or rustle |
| Reaction to a sharp throttle | A short yelp | Smooth change of pitch |
| With the belt off, turned by hand | Shows nothing | Roughness, play, notchy rotation |

The last row of the table is the decisive check. With the engine off, the
belt is removed by slackening the tensioner and each idler is turned by
hand in turn. A healthy one spins for a long time in silence; a sick one
feels rough, rustles or has play across its axis.

## Why it is worth telling them apart precisely

Replacing the belt without replacing a sick idler achieves nothing: the
squeal is back the next day, and with it the suspicion that the workshop
cheated. The opposite mistake costs money too — replacing a set of idlers
when the belt and correct tension would have done.

The logic of the check is the same as in other paired forks: first
separate the sounds by the conditions in which they appear, then confirm
by hand. The same approach is used for a hum at speed in
[humming: gearbox or wheel bearing](/en/articles/humming-gearbox-or-wheel-bearing/)
and for clicking in a turn in
[clicking: CV joint or brakes](/en/articles/clicking-cv-joint-or-brakes/).

## How the repair is decided

If the belt is already a few years old, it makes sense to replace it
together with the idlers in one go — getting back to the drive a second
time costs more than the difference in parts. While it is apart, ask for
the pulleys to be inspected too: worn grooves and a crankshaft pulley
running out of true eat the life of a new belt within months, and then the
squeal comes back through no fault of the workshop. The inspection takes
minutes and is usually included in the labour.

Two things mechanics do not always say. The tensioner is a consumable with
the same service life as the belt, and its spring is what actually holds
the tension — a quote listing only the belt is a quote that comes back.
And if there is oil or coolant on the pulleys, the leak has to be fixed
first: a new belt on contaminated pulleys repeats the story within days,
as covered in
[squealing after a belt change](/en/articles/belt-squeal-after-a-belt-change/).
