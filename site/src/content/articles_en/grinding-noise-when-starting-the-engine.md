Grinding at startup is metal on metal, and in most cases it is born in the
starter-and-flywheel pair. Less often the source turns out to be the
timing chain, the water pump or the camshaft phasers. When the sound
occurs — before the engine fires or after — immediately splits the causes
into two groups, so we start there, then cover the checks that need no
tools.

## Grinding before it fires: starter and ring gear

Until the engine starts, only one pair is turning: the starter's pinion
and the flywheel's ring gear. Grinding at that moment means the engagement
is going wrong:

- the pinion does not engage fully and skips over the tooth tips;
- the ring gear teeth are chewed — usually in patches, because the engine
  stops in the same positions, and then the grinding does not happen at
  every start;
- the pinion drive is worn and slips under load with a dry grinding
  sound;
- the starter is misaligned on its mounting after a recent removal — rare
  but real.

A separate scenario is grinding after the engine has fired, when the
pinion has not disengaged and the engine is spinning the starter. The
sound is loud and screeching, and it is a reason not to postpone the
repair: engine speed is far beyond what a starter can take. If instead of
cranking you only hear machine-gun chatter, start from
[the starter clicks but will not crank](/en/articles/starter-clicks-but-wont-crank/).

## Grinding after it fires: timing drive and accessories

If the starter has already gone quiet and a grinding overtone remains,
look in the engine itself and its accessories.

- **A timing chain with a tired tensioner** whips the guide for the first
  seconds — a rustling, grinding sound from the front cover that fades as
  oil pressure builds; see
  [signs of a failing timing chain tensioner](/en/articles/timing-chain-tensioner-failure-signs/)
  and
  [signs of a stretched timing chain](/en/articles/signs-of-a-stretched-timing-chain/).
- **Cam phasers** — the actuators that change valve timing — give a dry
  rattle rather than grinding, covered in
  [signs of a failing VVT actuator](/en/articles/vvt-actuator-failure-signs/).
- **The water pump** with a broken bearing grinds and whines constantly
  rather than only at startup, and often leaves coolant traces —
  [signs of a failing water pump](/en/articles/signs-of-a-failing-water-pump/).
- **Accessory belt idlers** with dried grease rustle and grind when cold
  until the bearings warm up.

The general map of cold engine sounds is on the symptom page
[engine knock when cold](/en/symptoms/engine-knock-when-cold/).

## Narrowing it down without tools

Three observations you can genuinely make on your driveway:

1. **A restart on a warm engine.** If the grinding has gone, hydraulics
   (chain, phasers) are likely; if it remains, the starter or the
   accessories.
2. **When the sound occurs.** Have a helper turn the key while you stand
   at the open bonnet: grinding before the engine fires lives in the
   starter, after it in the engine or the accessories.
3. **The character of the sound.** A thin squeal is not grinding but a
   slipping belt — that has its own page,
   [squeal on startup](/en/symptoms/squeal-on-startup/). Grinding is always
   metallic, with a grainy texture.

Those observations are usually enough for the workshop conversation to
start from a specific component rather than “something grinds somewhere”.

| When it grinds | Likely source | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| Before firing, while cranking | Pinion drive, ring gear | High: the ring gear is eaten fast |
| 1–3 seconds after firing | Timing chain, tensioner, phasers | Diagnosis at the next opportunity |
| Constantly while running | Water pump, idlers, alternator | Diagnosis this week |
| After firing, loud and screeching | The pinion has not disengaged | Workshop without delay |

## How the repair is decided

The economics are simple: the pinion drive is an inexpensive part; the
ring gear and the flywheel are not, because the gearbox has to come off to
reach them. The earlier the grinding is caught, the cheaper it is.

Two things worth asking. Whether the ring gear was actually inspected —
with the starter off it can be turned and looked at, and a workshop that
replaces a starter without doing so is guessing. And whether the starter
is being rebuilt or replaced: a worn pinion drive on an otherwise healthy
starter is a cheap repair, but only if the ring gear it meshes with is
sound, otherwise the new drive chews itself on the old teeth.

## What delay leads to

The pinion and ring gear wear each other: a chewed pinion eats the ring
gear and vice versa. In the worst case the starter one day simply fails to
engage and the car will not start away from home. Chain grinding is
dangerous differently: a stretched chain can jump a tooth, and that means
disturbed valve timing, lost power and, on many engines, the risk of
valves meeting pistons. Both scenarios develop over weeks and months, and
both are much cheaper to stop at the beginning.
