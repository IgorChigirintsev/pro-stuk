A squeal when the wheel is turned to full lock has a very characteristic
handwriting: it appears with the wheels at their extreme position, and
easing the wheel back quietens it. Almost always it is the voice of the
power steering: at the extreme point the pressure in the system is at its
maximum, and any weak link — the belt, the pump, the fluid — starts to
shout. Here are the causes by frequency and the simple checks under the
bonnet.

## Why it squeals at the stop

Hydraulic power steering is simple: a pump driven by the engine belt
circulates fluid and helps turn the wheels. Until the wheel reaches the
end of its travel, the pressure is moderate. At full lock the rack piston
meets its stop, the relief valve dumps the excess pressure, and the load
on the pump jumps to its maximum. From there, two scenarios: either the
belt cannot cope with the increased resistance and slips on the pulley — a
rubbery squeal — or a worn pump whines by itself. A short quiet squeak at
the stop is the working sound of the valve and is present on healthy cars
too. A loud squeal is already a symptom.

On cars with electric power steering there is no hydraulics, and there is
nothing to squeal at full lock: a similar sound there means a problem with
the accessory drive belt rather than the steering assistance —
[electric power steering fault signs](/en/articles/electric-power-steering-fault-signs/).

## Causes by frequency

| Sound | Likely cause | What to check |
|---|---|---|
| Sharp rubbery squeal | The drive belt is slipping | Tension, cracks, oil contamination |
| A whine turning into a squeal | Power steering pump wear | Hum during ordinary turns too |
| Squeal with a buzz, heavier steering | Low fluid, foam in the reservoir | Fluid level and colour |
| A one-second squeak at full lock | The relief valve working | Normal if quiet and short |

The belt leads the list. Over time it stretches, wears in the grooves and
stiffens; at full lock, when the pump needs maximum torque, the belt
breaks into slipping. The same squeal is often heard when the air
conditioning engages or in the rain — those cases are covered on the
symptom page
[belt squeal](/en/symptoms/belt-squeal/) and in
[alternator belt squeal: the causes](/en/articles/alternator-belt-squeal-causes/).

## Finding the cause in five minutes under the bonnet

1. **Check the power steering reservoir.** The level between the marks,
   the fluid clear, no burnt smell and no foam. Foam is a sign of air
   being drawn in: the pump whips the fluid and squeals.
2. **Inspect the belt.** Cracks across the ribs, a shiny glazed surface,
   traces of oil — any of those means replacement. Check the tensioner at
   the same time: a weak idler gives a squeal with a healthy belt,
   [a squeal: the belt or an idler bearing](/en/articles/squeal-belt-or-idler-bearing/).
3. **Listen to the pump.** If the hum is there on any turn and becomes a
   whine at full lock, the pump is worn — the detailed case is in
   [power steering whine when turning](/en/articles/power-steering-whine-when-turning/).
   It is important not to confuse that with running gear hum on the move:
   the full list of causes is on the symptom page
   [humming when turning](/en/symptoms/humming-when-turning/), and the
   cornering case in
   [humming when turning left](/en/articles/humming-when-turning-left/) and
   [humming when turning right](/en/articles/humming-when-turning-right/).

If the squeal comes with a knock or play in the steering, that is no
longer hydraulics but steering mechanics: the joints are checked
separately, and their signs are in
[signs of worn track rod ends](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-track-rod-ends/).

## How the repair is decided

The order pays to run from cheap to expensive: first the fluid level, then
the belt with its tensioner, and only then a verdict on the pump. Asking a
workshop to replace the pump without checking the belt is a classic way of
paying five times more than necessary.

Two things mechanics do not always say. If the fluid is dark or smells
burnt, replacing the pump without flushing the system sends the same
contamination straight through the new one — ask what happens to the fluid
and the reservoir filter. And if the level keeps falling, the leak has to
be found before anything is replaced: a fresh pump fed by a system that
drains itself repeats the failure.

## When the squeal is a reason to stop

A squeal by itself will not immobilise the car, but two scenarios call for
a quick reaction. First: the fluid level falls time after time — the
system is leaking, and one day the steering will suddenly go heavy on the
move. Second: on many cars the belt turns not only the power steering pump
but also the alternator and the water pump; breaking it on the road means
a flat battery and a risk of overheating, so a cracked belt is replaced
without waiting for it to snap —
[can you drive with a squealing belt](/en/articles/can-you-drive-with-a-squealing-belt/).
