Splines are the longitudinal teeth on a driveshaft where it enters the hub
or the gearbox. The joint transmits all the torque and is supposed to have
no clearance at all. When the teeth are crushed or worn, angular play
appears between the shaft and its mating part: at every change of load the
shaft first takes up that clearance and then strikes. Hence the
characteristic sound, which is often mistaken for a suspension fault.

## What it sounds like

The classic picture is a single dry click or dull knock at the moment the
load on the driveshaft changes sign. The most telling regimes:

- **Pulling away.** A click from the wheel side in the first metre, then
  silence.
- **Swapping throttle and brake.** Lift off, get back on the throttle and
  the knock repeats exactly at the transition.
- **Reverse after forward.** The play is taken up the other way and the
  click is especially clear.

The key difference from the suspension: the sound is not linked to bumps.
On smooth tarmac it is there; over a speed hump without a change of drive
it is not. Another marker: as wear grows the click becomes a double one
and starts coming with a slight jolt felt through the steering wheel.

## What is checked and how

The diagnosis is done on a lift with the wheel raised.

1. **Checking rotational play.** The wheel is held and the driveshaft is
   turned by hand back and forth: noticeable angular movement with the
   wheel stationary is a direct sign of a worn joint.
2. **Inspecting the boots.** A torn inner joint boot is often the cause of
   the wear that follows: the grease left, the dirt arrived.
3. **Checking the fit in the hub.** A loose or under-tightened hub nut
   accelerates spline damage, and it is the first thing checked.
4. **Separating it from the CV joint.** Circles on a car park at full
   lock: rattling in the turn is the joint, a click in a straight line as
   the drive changes is the splines — the joint's own signs are in
   [signs of a worn outer CV joint](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-outer-cv-joint/).

## What it gets confused with

| Source | When it appears | Character |
|---|---|---|
| Driveshaft splines | Change of drive, pulling away, reverse | Single click at the wheel |
| Outer CV joint | Tight turn under power | Chattering rattle, grows with angle |
| Engine mount | Pulling away, drive–reverse | Dull thump from the centre of the bay |
| Anti-roll bar link | Bumps | Dry knock in time with the bumps |
| Engine | Follows revs, not drive | Sound from under the bonnet, audible parked |

The last row is an important fork. If the knock follows engine revs rather
than the moment of pulling away, it is a different story altogether —
engine sounds like
[a stretched timing chain](/en/articles/what-a-stretched-timing-chain-sounds-like/),
or in a bad case worn bearings. Separating the groups is simple: stand
still and blip the throttle in neutral — whatever is audible then has
nothing to do with the driveshafts. A dull thump from the centre of the
engine bay belongs to
[engine mount failure](/en/articles/engine-mount-failure-symptoms/).

## How the repair is decided and how urgent it is

It is worth thinking about causes, not only consequences. Splines rarely
wear by themselves: they are usually helped along by a torn boot that let
water and sand into the joint, an under-tightened hub nut, wheelspin and
hard launches. So when a driveshaft is replaced it pays to check the state
of the other boots and the hub nut torque at the same time — otherwise the
new part follows the same script.

Two questions worth asking. Whether the hub nut will be tightened to the
specified torque with a torque wrench rather than an impact gun — that
single item decides whether the new joint lasts. And whether the wear was
found on the shaft, in the hub, or both: if the hub's splines are damaged
too, fitting a new shaft into them simply moves the problem.

The urgency depends on how much play there is. A barely audible click when
pulling away leaves a few thousand kilometres, but that is no reason to
forget it: the wear accelerates itself, because every impact enlarges the
clearance. A distinct double knock with a jolt is a reason to go to a
workshop this week.
