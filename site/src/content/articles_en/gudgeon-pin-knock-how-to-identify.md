A gudgeon pin is a steel cylinder joining the piston to the connecting rod
and passing on all the combustion pressure. The part is designed to last
the engine's life, so genuine pin knock is rare, and the phrase “the pins
are knocking” has been misapplied to detonation for decades. Nonetheless a
worn pin really does knock, its sound is recognisable, and it should not
be confused with other knocks: both the urgency and the budget are its
own. Here is how it appears, how it sounds and which tests confirm it.

## Where the play comes from

The pin is supported at three points: in two bosses in the piston and in
the small end of the connecting rod, usually through a bronze bush. Play
appears for three reasons:

- **oil starvation** — the small end bush is lubricated by splash and mist
  and is the first to wear when the level or pressure is low;
- **overheating** — the aluminium piston bosses lose their interference
  fit after a serious overheat;
- **detonation** — its shock waves batter the pin-to-boss fit, so advanced
  detonation eventually adds a genuine pin knock to its own pinging, as
  covered in
  [signs of engine detonation](/en/articles/signs-of-engine-detonation/).

A clearance of tenths of a millimetre is already audible: at every
reversal of force the pin shifts in its worn bore with a metallic click.

## What pin knock sounds like

The sound is high, sharp and dry — thinner than valvetrain ticking and
more distinct than detonation pinging. The main marker is its link to
changes of load: the knock is clearest at the moments when the force on
the piston reverses.

| Sign | Gudgeon pin | Detonation | Valves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Character | Sharp single click | A series of ringing “coins” | Even soft ticking |
| When heard | Lift-off and pick-up, throttle blips | Acceleration under load | Constantly, with revs |
| Dependence on fuel | None | Yes, octane rating | None |
| Warm-up | Barely affects it | Louder when hot | Quieter when hot |
| Cutting a cylinder | The knock changes | Unchanged | Unchanged |

The difference from detonation is fundamental: filling with higher-octane
fuel does nothing for a pin knock, while it usually kills detonation
pinging. The difference from piston slap is temperature: slap booms when
cold and fades after warm-up, a pin knocks the same way always. If your
knock lives only on a cold engine, start from
[is a cold knock that goes away dangerous](/en/articles/is-a-cold-knock-that-goes-away-dangerous/).

## The tests that confirm it

The home check is a throttle blip while parked: press and release the
throttle sharply on a warm engine. A pin answers with a sharp click
exactly at the changes of load — on pick-up and on lift-off. Then the same
test on a cold engine: if the sound is identical, the pin theory
strengthens; if it is louder cold, the piston assembly is more likely.

At a workshop the cylinders are cut one at a time: with the engine running
one cylinder's coil or injector is disconnected in turn. The load on that
piston falls, and a worn pin's knock changes noticeably or disappears —
that is how the specific cylinder is found. Then a compression test and an
endoscope inspection through the plug hole separate pin wear from bore
scoring, whose signs are in
[signs of cylinder bore scoring](/en/articles/signs-of-cylinder-bore-scoring/).
A dull low knock from deeper down is a different and more urgent story —
[signs of big-end bearing knock](/en/articles/signs-of-big-end-bearing-knock/).

## How the repair is decided

A pin cannot be replaced from outside: the engine has to come apart and
the piston assembly out. The pins, the small end bushes and, where the
bosses are damaged, the pistons are replaced, all with the cylinders
inspected. That is rebuild territory, and the important consequence is
timing: while the play is small the estimate is limited to pins and
bushes; hammered bosses add a set of pistons.

Two things worth settling with the workshop. Which cylinder was identified
and how — cutting cylinders is a five-minute test, and a diagnosis without
it is a guess. And whether the cause was found: oil starvation or
detonation that damaged the pin will damage the new one too.

Driving with a knocking pin is possible briefly and gently: no high revs,
no full loads. The play grows at an accelerating rate, and every kilometre
under load moves the repair towards the more expensive version.
