An injector is a solenoid valve that meters fuel: the needle lifts, the
fuel is injected, the needle seats again. Every cycle is a short click,
and on four cylinders there are thousands of clicks a minute. Merged
together they give an even ticking, clearly audible at an open bonnet.
Most often that is the normal operation of the fuel system, but there are
exceptions — here is how to tell one from the other.

## Why injectors tick, and tick differently

The loudness depends on the injection design. In port injection the
injectors sit in the inlet manifold and work at a few bar — the clicks are
quiet and almost lost against the engine. In direct injection (GDI on
Hyundai and Kia, TSI on Volkswagen, D-4 on Toyota) the fuel goes straight
into the cylinder at tens or hundreds of bar, so the injectors and the
high-pressure pump tick noticeably louder. Loudly enough that new owners
of such cars often take the standard sound for valve knock and drive to a
workshop.

At idle the ticking is more audible simply because the background is
quieter; as the revs rise it dissolves into the noise of the intake and
exhaust.

## When the ticking is normal

The signs of standard operation:

- the sound is even, in time with the engine, with no gaps or doubled
  clicks;
- the loudness is roughly the same day after day;
- the engine runs smoothly: stable idle, normal consumption, no check
  engine light;
- on direct injection the ticking is louder when cold and quietens with
  warm-up — that is normal too.

If all of that fits, the ticking can calmly be treated as the voice of a
healthy fuel system and ignored.

## When to take notice

A reason to check appears when the sound changes or gathers symptoms:

- the ticking has become noticeably louder or uneven — individual
  injectors falling out of rhythm;
- misfiring and shake at idle, flat spots on acceleration — the related
  causes are covered on the page
  [vibration at idle](/en/symptoms/vibration-at-idle/);
- consumption has risen and the exhaust smells of petrol;
- the check engine light is on with misfire or mixture codes.

The causes are deposits on the nozzles, wear of the needle and its seat, an
injector that over-delivers or, conversely, under-delivers. The mixture
then drifts from normal, and a badly lean mixture can ignite in the inlet
manifold — that scenario is covered in detail in
[intake backfire: the causes](/en/articles/intake-backfire-causes/).

## Telling injectors from other ticking parts

| Sign | Injectors | Hydraulic lifters |
|---|---|---|
| Dependence on warm-up | Almost none (GDI louder when cold) | Tick when cold, then quieten |
| Where the sound comes from | Fuel rail, top of the engine | The cam cover area |
| Character of the sound | Clear clicks in time | Soft, even ticking |
| Engine behaviour | Usually smooth | Smooth, the sound lives longer |

At a workshop the source is localised with a mechanical stethoscope in a
couple of minutes. Yourself, you can listen carefully through a long
screwdriver held against the fuel rail — but only against stationary parts
and well away from the fan and belts. When engine ticking is harmless and
when it is not is covered in
[engine ticking: dangerous or not](/en/articles/engine-ticking-dangerous-or-not/)
and on the symptom page
[engine ticking](/en/symptoms/ticking-engine/).

## Prevention and how the decision is made

The main allies of an injector are clean fuel and a healthy fuel filter,
which traps dirt before it reaches the nozzles; it should be changed on
schedule rather than when symptoms appear. Where there are signs of
contamination, cleaning the injectors helps.

What decides whether to clean anything is a scan tool, not the sound. The
fuel trims and misfire counters show whether the mixture has drifted and
which cylinder is lagging — and if both are within normal, the ticking is
the fuel system's normal voice and cleaning changes nothing. Ask for those
two figures by name before agreeing to a service.

One thing mechanics do not always say: on direct injection engines, carbon
on the inlet valves produces the same rough idle and flat spots as dirty
injectors, and no amount of injector cleaning touches it — that is a
separate job, and worth ruling in or out before paying for the wrong one.
