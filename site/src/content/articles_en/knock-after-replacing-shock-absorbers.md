You replace the dampers to get rid of a knock or float — and a new knock
sounds from under the wheels that was not there before. Annoying, but so
typical that it has a short list of causes: in the vast majority of cases
the fitting or the economised “small parts” are to blame rather than the
dampers themselves. Here is that list, from common to rare.

## Fitting mistakes are cause number one

The most widespread is **tightening the rod nut with an impact gun**. The
damper rod spins, its seat in the top mount is damaged, and the torque
ends up random. The correct method is to hold the rod with a separate
spanner and tighten the nut to torque. An under- or over-tightened nut
gives a clear knock from the top of the strut on every bump.

Other typical slips:

- the spring did not seat in its proper location during assembly — a knock
  and creak when the car is rocked;
- the strut-to-knuckle bolts or the top mount nuts are not tightened;
- spacers and washers from the mount kit are mixed up or forgotten.

All of these are cured by re-torquing and reassembly — quick, and under
the labour warranty.

## Economising on mounts and bump stops

A damper works inside its hardware: above it the top mount and its
bearing, on the rod the bump stop and the boot. Manufacturers recommend
replacing that hardware with the damper. If a new strut received an old
sagged mount or a crumbled bump stop, a knock is almost guaranteed: the
mount passes impacts into the body, and without a bump stop the suspension
bottoms out with a hard metallic strike — see
[signs of worn bump stops](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-bump-stops/).

The strut top mount bearing is its own story: its wear is heard not only
over bumps but when the steering is turned while parked, as covered in
[a noisy strut top mount bearing](/en/articles/strut-top-mount-bearing-noise-symptoms/).

## When the part itself is to blame

Faulty and counterfeit parts exist, especially for popular brands. The
signs: a knock from the first days with correct assembly, oil weeping on
the body, different firmness left and right. It is checked simply — by
swapping sides or replacing under warranty. The genuine defect rate at
established manufacturers is low, so this version is checked after the
fitting ones.

| Symptom | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Knock from above over small bumps | Rod nut, top mount, bearing | Re-torque, replace the mount |
| Hard impact in deep potholes | Missing or destroyed bump stop | Complete the strut hardware |
| Knock and creak when rocked | Spring not seated, dry bushes | Reassemble the strut |
| Knock from the first kilometres, oil weeping | A faulty damper | Warranty replacement |
| The knock existed before and got louder | A neighbouring component | Diagnosis |

## When the dampers are not involved

There is a third scenario: what knocks is not what was replaced. Strut
work almost always means undoing the anti-roll bar links, and a seized
joint is easily damaged in the process. Or more simply: new, firmer
dampers changed the suspension's behaviour, and wear in a neighbouring
component that was previously masked by float has become audible. You can
check that yourself — the method is in
[finding a suspension knock without a lift](/en/articles/finding-a-suspension-knock-without-a-lift/),
and the typical sounds are on the symptom pages
[knocking in the suspension](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-suspension/) and
[knocking over bumps](/en/symptoms/knocking-over-bumps/).

## The order of action

A sensible sequence:

1. Drive 300–500 km and return to the workshop for a check re-torque —
   normal practice, not a complaint.
2. If the knock remains, ask for the rod nut torque and the state of the
   mounts and bump stops to be checked on a lift.
3. Disputed cases are settled by an independent diagnosis.

The conversation with a workshop is easier when there is a work order and
a clear history: what was replaced, when the sound appeared. The wider
version of that conversation is in
[a knock after suspension work: who is to blame](/en/articles/knock-after-suspension-repair/).
