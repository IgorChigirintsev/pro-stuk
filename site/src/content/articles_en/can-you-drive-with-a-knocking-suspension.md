The short answer: it depends on what is knocking. The same “knock in the
suspension” can mean a cheap anti-roll bar bush you can happily drive on
until a scheduled visit — or a ball joint with a folded wheel ahead of it.
The driver's job is not to make an exact diagnosis but to judge the
urgency correctly. For that it is enough to know which parts tolerate
neglect, which do not, and how they sound.

## The risk map by part

| Part | Can you drive? | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-roll bar links and bushes | Yes, repair as planned | Comfort, steering precision in turns |
| Dampers | Limited | Stopping distance, float, wear on neighbours |
| Control arm bushes | Not for long | The car wandering, angled tyre wear |
| Strut top mount | Limited | Heavier steering, growing knock |
| Ball joint | Only as far as the workshop | Failure folds the wheel under |
| Track rod ends and rods | Only as far as the workshop | Steering play, loss of control |

The table gives a first bearing, but parts rarely introduce themselves by
name — so here is how to tell them apart by ear.

## What is relatively tolerant

The **anti-roll bar** — the springy bar that reduces body roll — is the
most harmless source of knock. Its links and bushes give a frequent dull
knock over fine ripples and speed humps. A car with a worn anti-roll bar
stays controllable — it simply rolls more and floats in fast corners. What
happens if you leave the links is in
[what happens if you do not replace anti-roll bar links](/en/articles/not-replacing-anti-roll-bar-links/).

**Control arm bushes and dampers** are the “not for long” category. A worn
damper does not keep the wheel on the road: the stopping distance grows,
the car floats, and every impact goes into the bushes and the wheel
bearings. You can drive, but the count is in weeks rather than months, and
without sharp manoeuvres on broken surfaces — the details are in
[driving with dead shock absorbers](/en/articles/driving-with-dead-shock-absorbers/).

## What is not tolerant

The **ball joint** is the pivot that both holds and steers the wheel. Its
play grows unpredictably: from the first knock to failure can be six
months or one good pothole. The ending is always the same — the pin comes
out of its housing and the wheel folds under. At parking speed that is a
tow truck and a bent wing; on a motorway it is loss of control. Why it
matters is set out in
[what a broken ball joint means](/en/articles/ball-joint-failure-danger/),
and the self-check in
[how to check a ball joint yourself](/en/articles/how-to-check-a-ball-joint-yourself/).

**Track rod ends and rods** follow the same logic: play in the steering is
no longer a noise but a direct risk. The signs are a knock over small
bumps felt through the wheel, play in the steering, and the car wandering
in a straight line. The check is in
[checking track rod ends for play](/en/articles/checking-track-rod-ends-for-play/).

The economics are simple: a suspension inspection on a lift is one of the
cheapest things a workshop does, and it is often free when you book the
repair with it. Waiting until you need a tow truck is the most expensive
way to save on a check.

## Judging urgency by sound

- **frequent small knocking over ripples**, unchanged in turns — the
  anti-roll bar signature: repair as planned, within the month;
- **single dull thumps over potholes** — damper or control arm bushes:
  diagnosis within a week or two;
- **a knock that changes in turns, or comes with a creak as you steer** —
  ball joint suspected: workshop within days, drive gently;
- **a knock felt through the steering wheel, or steering play** — the
  steering: same again, and no speed;
- **any knock that has suddenly got louder** — urgent: fast growth means
  fast wear.

## What definitely not to do

Do not drown the knock out with music — sound is the only free condition
sensor the suspension has. Do not replace parts “on suspicion” without
checking for play: that way half the running gear gets renewed and the
thing knocking turns out to be the undertray. And do not put the diagnosis
off until the next service if the sound changes from week to week: a
stable knock can wait, a growing one cannot.
