Scoring means deep vertical scratches on the cylinder bore — the
mirror-smooth surface the piston slides on. Once they appear they do not
go away: the oil film tears at that point, wear accelerates, and the
engine gradually heads for a rebuild. The good news is that scoring almost
always warns you in advance — the point is to hear it.

## Where scoring comes from

The mechanism is always the same: the oil film tears where the piston
meets the wall, and metal rubs on metal. There are several reasons:

- **oil starvation** — a low level, infrequent changes, a blocked oil
  filter, or running worn-out oil in hot weather;
- **overheating** — the piston expands beyond its design and presses
  against the wall;
- **abrasive** — ceramic dust from a disintegrating catalytic converter
  drawn back into the cylinders, or dust that got past a poor air filter;
- **design characteristics** — some engines are prone to scoring even with
  careful use.

## Five signs of scoring

1. **A knock when cold.** The earliest and most characteristic sign: a
   dull diesel-like knock for the first minutes after starting that fades
   as the engine warms. The piston with its worn skirt rocks in the bore
   until it expands with heat.
2. **Rising oil consumption.** The scratches let oil into the combustion
   chamber: first a top-up every few thousand kilometres, then not making
   it from one service to the next.
3. **Metallic dust in the oil.** A glitter on the dipstick, swarf on the
   magnetic plug or in the old filter at a change.
4. **Rough running and lost power.** Compression in the affected cylinder
   falls and the engine starts misfiring, especially when cold.
5. **Blue smoke on a throttle blip.** Burning oil gives the exhaust a
   bluish tint, noticeable when the revs are raised sharply from idle.

No sign alone is a verdict, but two or three together are solid grounds
for an endoscopy without delay.

## The stages: from first scratches to a rebuild

| Stage | What you hear and see | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Early | A light knock when cold for 1–3 minutes | Endoscopy, monitor the oil |
| Middle | The knock lasts longer, oil topped up between services | Prepare a repair budget |
| Advanced | Knock when warm too, misfiring, smoke | Repair soon |
| Critical | Constant knock, swarf in the oil | Stop using the car |

The rate of progression is unpredictable, but the direction is always the
same. The closer to the lower rows, the more relevant the question in
[can you drive with engine knock](/en/articles/can-you-drive-with-engine-knock/) —
at an advanced stage every cold start takes life out of the engine. Where
the line runs between a harmless cold noise and a warning is in
[is a cold knock that goes away dangerous](/en/articles/is-a-cold-knock-that-goes-away-dangerous/).

## Which engines are at risk

Certain petrol engines are known for it, and their owners' forums are
full of the same story: a cold knock, then oil consumption, then an
endoscope. A collapsed catalytic converter is a risk of its own on engines
where it sits close to the manifold — the ceramic dust works as an
abrasive, as covered in
[a broken catalytic converter: the sound](/en/articles/broken-catalytic-converter-rattle/).
If your car belongs to a model with a known reputation, make it a rule: an
endoscopy once a year, or at any new cold-start knock. Compare your sound
with the typical recordings on the symptom pages
[engine knock when cold](/en/symptoms/engine-knock-when-cold/) and
[knocking in the engine](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-engine/).

## Confirming it and what follows

The order of confirmation: a compression test cylinder by cylinder, then
an endoscopy through the spark plug holes. Together they take about an
hour. If scoring is confirmed, the repair options are re-boring the block
to an oversize or sleeving it with new pistons. That is a rebuild, and
there are no intermediate cures: additives and decarbonising do not remove
scratches from metal.

Two questions decide the job. First, whether the cause was found: if the
scoring came from a disintegrating converter or an oil starvation problem,
a rebuilt engine meets the same conditions. Second, whether the bearings
were inspected at the same time — the debris the scoring has already
produced circulates with the oil, and a rebuild that ignores it is a short
one. If a dull knock from deep in the block has joined in, that is a
different and more urgent question —
[signs of big-end bearing knock](/en/articles/signs-of-big-end-bearing-knock/).
