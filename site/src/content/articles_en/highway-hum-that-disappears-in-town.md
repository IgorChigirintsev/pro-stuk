A whole class of complaints looks like this: around town the car behaves
impeccably, and on the motorway past eighty an even low hum appears that
makes conversation in the cabin difficult. You arrive at a workshop, the
mechanic drives a lap of the yard and shrugs. This is common and quite
explicable: in town the conditions that make the sound audible simply are
not met. Here is why, and how to catch the source without driving out of
town every time.

## Why speed decides everything

For most rotating parts, the frequency of the sound is directly
proportional to speed. A wheel around 65 centimetres across turns about
eight times a second at 60 km/h and sixteen times at 120 km/h. A defect in
a bearing raceway that produces a dull murmur at the edge of hearing at
low frequency becomes a distinct howl at double the frequency.

The second factor is the acoustic background. In town, engine noise at
changing revs, the gearbox and outside sounds dominate. On the motorway
the engine runs evenly and quietly, the speed is constant, and your
hearing gets ideal conditions to pick out one continuous tone. That is why
a “motorway” hum often turns out to be a problem that existed in town as
well — nobody could hear it.

## Three groups of sources

| Source | How it behaves | The check |
|---|---|---|
| Wheel bearing | Grows smoothly from 60 km/h upwards | Gentle lane changes left and right |
| Tyres, sawtooth wear | Even hum, changes with the surface | Run a hand across the tread |
| Transmission, differential | Follows drive, not only speed | Compare under power and coasting |
| Aerodynamics, door seals | Appears abruptly at its own speed | Tape the joint over and drive it |
| Undertray, arch liner | Narrow band, rattling edge | Look underneath, shake it by hand |

The order of checks fits into a single drive. First the coasting test:
accelerate to the speed where the hum lives and select neutral. If the hum
stays, the source rotates with the wheels; if it changes, look at the
transmission. Then the lane-change test: ease left, then right. A change
in volume points at a wheel bearing, and the one that gets louder is the
side the weight moves onto. Finally the surface test: drive on smooth and
on coarse tarmac. Tyres change their voice; a bearing does not.

## When it is not a bearing at all

There is a characteristic sign that rules out the chassis: the hum appears
and disappears abruptly within a narrow band of speeds. That behaviour is
typical of resonance. A door seal that has come away, a loose engine
undertray, a slack arch liner or a roof rail starts vibrating at its own
frequency, hums, and falls silent as soon as speed leaves the band. It is
easy to test: tape the suspect joint over with masking tape and drive the
same stretch.

A second scenario is not about the hum itself but what sits next to it. If
individual clicks in time with wheel rotation have joined the hum, that is
a different symptom. The general catalogue of speed-related sounds is on
the symptom page
[humming while driving](/en/symptoms/humming-while-driving/).

## Talking to a workshop about it

The practical advice is not to describe the hum in words but to name the
conditions. “It appears after 80 km/h, grows to 120, does not go away when
coasting, and is louder when I ease left” tells a mechanic where to look
straight away, without wasting a lap of the yard.

Two things are worth knowing about the repair if a wheel bearing is
confirmed. Whether your car uses a separate bearing pressed into the hub
or a complete hub assembly decides both the price and the length of the
job. And the hub nut torque matters more here than on most work — an
over- or under-tightened nut destroys a new bearing within thousands of
kilometres, so a torque wrench rather than an impact gun is what you want
to see.

The main difficulty with this kind of hum is that you cannot demonstrate
it to a mechanic: it only exists on the motorway. Record it in the Stuk
app while driving at the speed where it lives and during lane changes —
the app matches the recording with your answers and shows the likely
causes with percentages, and the recording itself can simply be played to
the mechanic.
