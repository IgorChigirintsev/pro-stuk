One quiet clunk at the moment you move off and another as you stop is a
complaint workshops hear constantly. The sound is annoying in its
regularity, yet the car can be perfectly quiet over bumps. That is an
important clue: what is knocking is not the suspension in the classic
sense but some component whose play shifts when the direction of force
changes. Here is where to look for it.

## Why the sound is single

When you pull away, drive presses every part with a clearance one way;
when you brake, the other way. If there is free movement somewhere, it is
taken up once — with a short thump. After that the parts stay pressed and
the sound does not repeat until the direction of force changes again. So
the clunk is heard strictly when moving off, stopping and shifting between
drive and reverse — and almost never in the middle of steady driving.

## Brakes: the most common cause

Top of the statistics is the pads shifting in the caliper. Pads sit in the
carrier's guides with a small clearance; when the direction of disc
rotation changes they slide to the stop and click. Over time the clearance
grows: the guides wear, the anti-rattle shims — the spring plates that
hold the pad — are lost or flatten. The sound gets louder and starts
appearing over small bumps too.

The check takes a minute: shuttle between drive and reverse a few times
while parked and the clunk repeats; pull away with the brake held lightly
and it disappears. The cure is servicing the caliper: cleaning, greasing
the slide pins, new shims and, if needed, pads. If the caliper is also
sticking, the checks are in
[how to check a brake caliper for sticking](/en/articles/how-to-check-a-brake-caliper-for-sticking/).

## Driveline, mounts and suspension

If the brake test gives nothing, the circle widens:

- **engine and gearbox mounts** — the rubber blocks the powertrain hangs
  on. A worn mount lets the engine rock when you pull away with a dull
  thump; the same knock is heard on gear changes and sharp throttle — see
  [knocking when accelerating](/en/symptoms/knocking-when-accelerating/)
  and the load test in
  [checking engine mounts under load](/en/articles/checking-engine-mounts-under-load/);
- **propshaft universal joints and splines** on rear- and four-wheel-drive
  cars — a metallic clunk from below, under the tunnel;
- **trailing arm bushes** — less often: they usually knock over potholes
  as well;
- **loose wheel bolts** — rare, but compulsory to check.

| Source | When it is heard | Quick check |
|---|---|---|
| Pads in the caliper | Moving off, stopping, D–R | No clunk with the brake held |
| Caliper slide pins | The same plus small bumps | Caliper service on a lift |
| Engine mount | Moving off, D–R, sharp throttle | Engine rocking on a throttle blip |
| Universal joint | Moving off, sound under the floor | Propshaft play by hand on a lift |
| Control arm bushes | Moving off/braking plus potholes | Inspect the joint rubber |
| Wheel bolts | Clunk from one specific wheel | Torque check with a wheel brace |

## How the repair is decided

Work from cheap to expensive: wheel bolts, then the calipers, and only
then the mounts and driveline. That order is not just about money — it is
about not replacing a mount because of a pad that costs nothing to shim.

Two things are worth insisting on with brakes. First, that the slide pins
are actually stripped, cleaned and re-greased with the correct high
temperature grease rather than “looked at” — dry pins are the single most
common reason the clunk comes back a month later. Second, that new
anti-rattle shims go in with the pads; they are usually included in a
decent pad set and often left in the box.

With mounts, the question to ask is which one was found to be moving and
how. A mount is judged under load, with someone applying drive against the
brake while the engine bay is watched — not by eye with the engine off.

## If the clunk appeared after a repair or in the cold

Two special cases. First: the clunk appeared right after suspension or
brake work — then go back to the same workshop for a re-torque, which is
normal practice. Second: the sound only lives in cold weather and goes
after warm-up — stiffened rubber in mounts and joints is to blame. If a
single clunk gradually turns into a constant knock over every bump, that
is the profile of the symptom page
[knocking in the suspension](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-suspension/).
