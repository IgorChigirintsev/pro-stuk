The G4FC is the most common 1.6 petrol engine in earlier Hyundai Solaris
and Kia Rio generations, and one of the most frequent complaints about it
sounds like this: it ticks at idle. The phrase alarms people because
online the word "ticking" has become welded to the words "bore scoring".
In practice the same timbre can have a perfectly standard explanation or
be a reason for an endoscope. Here is where the line runs.

## What ticks on this engine as standard

The G4FC is a naturally aspirated engine with port injection, and part of
its sound picture is built in by design. Port injectors click their
solenoid valves several times a second per cylinder; with the bonnet open
that is heard as an even dry chatter, especially at the front of the
cylinder head. The valve train adds its own contribution. That sound has
characteristic markers: it is even in loudness, does not grow with warm-up,
does not increase under load and barely comes through into the cabin with
the bonnet shut. The general case is covered in
[ticking injectors: normal or not](/en/articles/ticking-injectors-normal-or-not/).

There are also external sources wrongly attributed to the engine: a
chattering accessory belt idler, a noisy alternator, a rattling exhaust
manifold heat shield. They are separated simply — by whether the sound
changes with the drive belt removed and when the shield is tapped.

## The signs that move the conversation away from normal

| What you hear | What it resembles | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| Even chatter, stable through warm-up | Injectors, valve train | Usually normal |
| Bright ticking louder when cold, quieter warm | Clearances in the valve drive | Adjustment / timing check |
| Rattle at start-up and on lift-off | Chain stretch, worn tensioner | Timing drive diagnosis |
| Dull knock in time with revs when hot, under load | Piston group | Compression, endoscope |
| Pinging on acceleration under load | Detonation, fuel, ignition | Plugs, fuel, sensors |

The behaviour of the sound through warm-up is worth listening to
separately. Many mechanical clearances are larger on a cold engine, so
ticking at start-up that fades after a couple of minutes is a common and
usually uncritical picture. It is worse when the sound gets louder on a
warm engine rather than quieter. The general logic for separating such
sounds is covered in
[an engine knock at idle](/en/articles/engine-knock-at-idle/) and
[an engine knock when hot](/en/articles/engine-knock-when-hot/), with the
sound portraits on the symptom page
[engine ticking](/en/symptoms/ticking-engine/).

## Where the bore scoring talk comes from

Bore scoring is damage to the cylinder surface — marks on the wall that
cause oil consumption to rise, compression to fall and a dull knock to
appear. On this engine family two mechanisms are usually discussed. The
first is abrasive particles reaching the cylinders; in particular, the
theory of catalytic converter ceramic breaking up and being drawn back
into the combustion chamber is widely debated. The second is ordinary
operating conditions: overheating, poor fuel, late oil changes, regular
cold starts under high load.

It is important to stay sober: ticking on its own does not prove scoring.
The diagnosis is made by measurement rather than by sound — compression,
measured oil consumption, and an endoscope inspection of the cylinders
through the plug holes. If those checks are clean, the sound almost
certainly belongs to the standard texture or to the accessories. The full
symptom list is in
[signs of cylinder bore scoring](/en/articles/signs-of-cylinder-bore-scoring/).
Similar logic applies to the chain drive: the signs of wear are listed in
[signs of a stretched timing chain](/en/articles/signs-of-a-stretched-timing-chain/).

## How the decision is made

The gap between an oil change and a rebuild is exactly why a diagnosis
should not be made from a forum thread. The sensible order is: first
record the sound and note the conditions in which it appears, then measure
oil consumption over a couple of thousand kilometres, and only then, if
the figures are worrying, go for an endoscope.

Two things mechanics do not always say. Oil consumption is the single most
informative number here, and it has to be measured properly — level
checked on the same level ground, at the same temperature, with the
mileage between checks written down; impressions are worthless. And an
endoscope inspection is cheap compared with everything it rules out, so
ask for the photographs of the bores rather than a verbal verdict.
