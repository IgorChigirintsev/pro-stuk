An idler pulley is a small part nobody thinks about until the engine bay
starts howling. Its job is simple: to route the accessory belt along the
path it needs, around the alternator, air conditioning and water pump
pulleys. Inside the idler is an ordinary ball bearing, and it ages the way
any bearing does — first the timbre changes, then vibration appears, and
finally the unit seizes. Here is how that wear sounds, how to separate it
from similar noises, and what decides the repair.

## What a worn idler sounds like

The first sign is an even whine or hum tied to engine revs rather than
road speed. With the car parked, raise the revs smoothly: the sound rises
and falls with the tachometer needle. At idle it often reads as a quiet
rustle or “sand shuffling”; at 2,000–2,500 rpm it turns into a clear
whine.

The second sign is a dependence on temperature and moisture. While the
grease in the bearing is thick, the sound is louder in the morning; after
warm-up it can almost disappear and come back in the evening. Noise
appearing after a wash or after driving through deep water is
characteristic too: water washes out what grease is left.

The third is a metallic edge. When the bearing cage starts to break up,
the even whine turns into a rumble with occasional clicks. That is a late
stage and is best not reached. The full range of shades of this sound is
on the symptom page [belt squeal](/en/symptoms/belt-squeal/).

## Idler, belt or alternator

| Source | Character of the sound | Distinguishing feature |
|---|---|---|
| Idler / tensioner pulley | Even whine, hum, rustle | Ignores load, audible at idle |
| Drive belt | Sharp squeal, screech | Grows with the a/c on and when starting |
| Alternator bearing | Hum with a metallic edge | Grows with lights and heaters on |
| Water pump | Low hum, sometimes with weeping | Often with coolant traces on the pulley |
| Alternator decoupler pulley | Rattle, chatter on lift-off | Disappears at steady revs |

Telling them apart saves money: a squealing belt is cured by a belt, while
an idler's whine is not cured by a belt at all. The alternator-versus-pump
fork is covered in
[whine under the bonnet: alternator or water pump](/en/articles/alternator-or-water-pump-whine/),
the pump check in
[a whining water pump: how to check it](/en/articles/water-pump-whine-how-to-check/),
and noises that appear only with the air conditioning on in
[A/C compressor clutch: the sounds of failure](/en/articles/ac-compressor-clutch-failure-sounds/).

## How to check it yourself

The most reliable check is done with the engine off. The belt is removed
by releasing the tensioner, and each pulley is turned by hand in turn. A
healthy one spins for a long time, silently and without noticeable
resistance. A failing one gives itself away with crunching, a notchy feel,
rustling or visible play if you rock it across its axis.

If you would rather not remove the belt, an indirect test works: with the
engine running and your hands and clothing well clear of the rotating
parts, hold a long screwdriver or a hose against the idler's housing with
the other end to your ear. That makes an improvised stethoscope and finds
the source in a minute — the versions that work are described in
[a mechanic's stethoscope you can make yourself](/en/articles/diy-mechanics-stethoscope/).

## How the repair is decided

The practical rule is simple: if the belt has covered noticeable mileage,
the idlers are replaced with it rather than one at a time. The difference
in the cost of parts is smaller than the cost of removing and refitting
the belt a second time.

Two things a workshop does not always mention. First, an automatic
tensioner has a wear indicator marking that shows whether it is still
within range — a new belt on an out-of-range tensioner will squeal again.
Second, on engines with a stretch-fit belt there is no tensioner at all
and the belt requires a special fitting tool; a belt levered on with a
screwdriver is damaged from the first minute even if it does not show.

Accessory whine is one of the rare cases where the sound barely lies and
the diagnosis rests on one or two signs. What a snapped belt costs is set
out in
[can you drive with a squealing belt](/en/articles/can-you-drive-with-a-squealing-belt/).
