The subject of cylinder bore scoring has become firmly attached to Hyundai
and Kia engines — so firmly that any stray sound from under the bonnet is
reflexively fitted to it by owners of a Solaris, Rio, Sportage or Tucson.
The reality is calmer: most knocks have other, far cheaper explanations.
But scoring does have its own sound signature, and it is useful to know —
if only to understand when it is time to move from listening to measuring.

## What scoring is and why it makes noise

Scoring is damage to the cylinder wall: grooves and torn areas on the
surface the piston travels along. Because of them the rings lose their
seal, compression falls and oil starts passing into the combustion
chamber. The clearance between piston and wall grows, the piston gains
freedom to rock as it passes top dead centre — and at that moment the
skirt strikes the wall. That produces the sound mechanics call piston
slap: dull, muffled, in time with the revs, closer to the bottom of the
block.

The causes of the damage vary: abrasive material reaching the cylinder,
oil starvation, overheating, prolonged running under detonation, poor
fuel, over-long oil change intervals. In practice one factor rarely acts
alone — usually several overlap.

## The sound portrait

| Sign | Bore scoring | Other common causes |
|---|---|---|
| Timbre | Dull, muffled knock | Ticking, ringing, chatter do not fit |
| Rhythm | In time with revs, even | Injectors click faster and finer |
| Warm-up | Early stage quieter when hot, late stage does not go | Valve clearances nearly always fade |
| Load | More noticeable under load and on acceleration | Accessories usually ignore load |
| Companion signs | Oil consumption, loss of pull, smoke | Accessories and timing drive have none |

The key sign is not the knock itself but the company it keeps. Scoring
almost never arrives alone: along with the sound, the oil topped up
between changes increases, bluish smoke appears on a sharp throttle after
idling, acceleration loses its flexibility, the engine holds its revs less
well. A sound with none of those companions far more often turns out to be
the chain, the valve train or the accessories. The logic for separating
those cases is covered in
[an engine knock at idle](/en/articles/engine-knock-at-idle/), and the
general catalogue of this group of sounds is on the symptom page
[knocking in the engine](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-engine/), with the full
symptom list in
[signs of cylinder bore scoring](/en/articles/signs-of-cylinder-bore-scoring/).

## How scoring is distinguished from similar knocks

The first thing ruled out is the timing drive. A stretched chain gives a
rattle and a rustle at start-up and on lift-off rather than an even
impact under load; its signs are listed in
[signs of a stretched timing chain](/en/articles/signs-of-a-stretched-timing-chain/).
The second is the valve train: its ticking is bright and high, heard from
above at the cylinder head, and usually goes with warm-up. The third is
the accessories: if the drive belt is removed and the sound disappears,
the piston group has nothing to do with it.

Then the measuring starts. A compression test shows whether one or two
cylinders are down. An endoscope through a plug hole lets you see the
cylinder wall and tell light marks from deep grooves. It is also useful to
work out the real oil consumption: a top-up tied to mileage is a far more
honest indicator than impressions. The related model-specific case is in
[G4FC ticking: normal or bore scoring](/en/articles/g4fc-engine-ticking-normal-or-bore-scoring/).

## How the decision is made

The gap between a diagnosis and a rebuild is enormous here, and that is
exactly why you should neither diagnose yourself from an internet video
nor drag it out to the last. The sensible sequence is to record the sound,
track the oil consumption over the next thousand kilometres, then have a
compression test and an endoscope inspection. Those three steps cost
incomparably less than a mistake in either direction.

Two things mechanics do not always say. Oil consumption has to be measured
properly to mean anything — the level checked on the same level ground, at
the same temperature, with the mileage between checks written down. And
ask for the endoscope photographs rather than a verbal verdict: the
difference between light honing marks and deep grooves is what decides
between watching and opening the engine, and it is visible in a picture.
