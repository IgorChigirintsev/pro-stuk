After suspension work a car should be quieter than before it. If instead a
new knock has appeared, there are exactly three versions: a fitting
mistake, a faulty part, or a component that was not part of the job. Each
version has its own signature and its own timescale, so “who is to blame”
can usually be established before anything is dismantled. Here they are in
order.

## Version one: a fitting mistake

The most common error when replacing arms and bushes is **tightening with
the suspension hanging**. A bush is a rubber sleeve that works in torsion:
it must be tightened with the car on its wheels and the arms in their
working position. If the fasteners were tightened on a lift with the
wheels hanging, the rubber is permanently twisted, tears within one or two
thousand kilometres — and the component knocks as though it had never been
replaced. That mechanism is covered in
[signs of worn suspension bushes](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-suspension-bushes/).

Other fitting causes: an under-tightened fastener (a knock from the first
kilometres), tightening by feel without a torque wrench, forgotten washers
and spacers. In the same category is a skipped alignment: it does not
knock, but it gives itself away with the car pulling and angled tyre wear.

## Version two: the part

Aftermarket shelves carry both fakes of well-known brands and outright
no-name parts. They look identical, but their life differs several-fold: a
counterfeit ball joint or track rod end can start knocking within
5,000–10,000 km even with perfect fitting.

The signature of this version: it is the replaced component that knocks,
but not immediately — after thousands of kilometres — and the play is
confirmed on a lift.

## Version three: the repair has nothing to do with it

Suspensions wear as a set. While a broken arm was knocking, it masked its
neighbours; after the replacement the car became more composed — and
against the new quiet, the next component in line showed itself, most
often the anti-roll bar links. Their signature is in
[signs of worn anti-roll bar links](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-anti-roll-bar-links/).
Pure coincidence happens too: an undertray fastener that came loose a week
after the repair rings metallically and is easily taken for a suspension
knock — the difference is in
[metallic clatter under the car over bumps](/en/articles/metallic-clatter-under-the-car-over-bumps/).

## Telling the versions apart

| Sign | Likely version |
|---|---|
| A knock from day one, on every small bump | Under-tightened fastener |
| A knock 1,000–2,000 km after new bushes | Tightened with the suspension hanging |
| The replaced part knocks again after 5,000–10,000 km | Part quality |
| It knocks elsewhere, or with a different character | A neighbouring component; the repair is not involved |
| Metallic rattling from below | Undertray or shield fasteners |

The general picture of suspension sounds is on the symptom pages
[knocking in the suspension](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-suspension/) and
[rattling under the car](/en/symptoms/rattling-under-car/) — worth checking
before formulating a complaint. Creaks that came with the knock have their
own breakdown in
[creaking when you rock the car](/en/articles/creaking-when-you-rock-the-car/).

## How to resolve it with the workshop

The first step is to go back to where the work was done, calmly and
without accusations: labour usually carries a warranty, and a re-torque or
reassembly under it is free. State the complaint specifically: where it
knocks, at what speed, on which bumps, whether the sound has changed over
time.

If the workshop insists everything is fine while the knock objectively
exists, an independent diagnosis at another workshop gives you a written
argument: which joint has play and whether it relates to the recent work.
With the work order and that assessment the question is almost always
resolved without conflict: a decent workshop finds it easier to redo the
job than to argue.

The strongest argument in that conversation is the sound itself. Record it
in the Pro-Stuk app on a representative stretch of road: the algorithm matches
the recording with your answers and shows the likely sources with an
urgency level. With that result it is easier both to talk to the workshop
and to judge how much of a hurry you are actually in.
