The alternator is the fastest-turning unit on the accessory drive: its
pulley is two to three times smaller than the crankshaft pulley, so the
rotor spins to tens of thousands of revolutions per minute. It runs on two
rolling bearings, and when they wear the alternator becomes the main
source of foreign noises under the bonnet. The good news: those noises
have a recognisable signature, and the suspicion can be tested without
dismantling anything.

## Why alternator bearings wear

There are several causes of accelerated wear, and almost all of them are
external. Belt tension presses on the rotor shaft with a constant side
load — if the belt is over-tightened, the load on the front bearing
multiplies. From below the alternator catches water and dirt from the
road: deep puddles and pressure-washing the engine wash the grease out.
From above the exhaust manifold heats it — heat thins and ages the grease
faster.

Add the belt jerks from the crankshaft's uneven rotation, which the
decoupler pulley is supposed to absorb; a worn decoupler does the
opposite and passes the shocks straight into the shaft. So the condition
of the decoupler and the bearings is directly linked.

## What the wear sounds like

The sound develops in stages, and it can be caught at each of them:

1. **A thin whistle when cold.** The first minutes after starting, fading
   as it warms — the grease has thickened and is not covering the
   raceways. A slipping belt gives a similar sound; the difference is on
   the symptom page [belt squeal](/en/symptoms/belt-squeal/).
2. **A whine that rises with revs.** The most recognisable stage: a tonal
   sound that climbs when you blip the throttle and grows noticeably with
   the headlights and heated screen on — electrical load presses the rotor
   harder.
3. **Hum and rumble at any revs.** The raceways are broken; the sound is
   there at idle and on the move, and does not follow road speed.
4. **Grinding and a metallic rustle.** The cage is disintegrating — seizure
   is a few journeys away.

The key marker at every stage: the sound follows engine revs rather than
road speed, and does not change in turns — unlike a wheel bearing. A
model-specific example is in
[Chevrolet Cruze: alternator bearing whine](/en/articles/chevrolet-cruze-alternator-bearing-whine/).

## How to check without dismantling

Four techniques available in a garage:

- **The load test.** At idle, switch on the main beam, the heated rear
  screen and the blower. A whine that grows is strong evidence for the
  alternator.
- **A stethoscope.** The tip on the alternator casing with the engine
  running — a worn bearing is heard immediately. A long screwdriver with
  the handle to your ear works too; keep clear of rotating parts. The
  home-made versions are described in
  [a mechanic's stethoscope you can make yourself](/en/articles/diy-mechanics-stethoscope/).
- **Running without the belt.** A brief start with the accessory belt
  removed: if the sound disappears, an accessory is to blame; if it
  remains, look deeper into the engine.
- **Checking for play.** With the engine off, rock the alternator pulley
  across its axis by hand: a perceptible knock or play means the bearings
  are broken.

## What it gets confused with

| Sign | Alternator bearing | Decoupler pulley | Belt idler | Water pump |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reaction to lights and heaters | Grows | None | None | None |
| Whistle on start-up and shutdown | Rarely | Characteristic | Possible | Possible |
| Rattle after switching off | No | Characteristic | No | No |
| Coolant weeping | No | No | No | Characteristic |

## How the repair is decided

Alternator bearings are a consumable, and replacing them is better value
than replacing the whole unit; a new alternator costs several times a
rebuild, so overhauling is standard practice.

The questions worth asking before agreeing to the job are these. What else
comes out of it — brushes, the regulator, the decoupler — since the
alternator is already off and the labour is paid for. Whether the belt
tension was checked, because an over-tightened belt is what killed the old
bearings and will kill the new ones. And whether the alternator's own
cooling path is clear: a unit clogged with dirt runs hotter and cooks its
grease.

There is one reason not to drag it out: a seized bearing stops the whole
belt drive. The car instantly loses charging, and on many engines the
water pump and the power steering too. What was a booked repair becomes a
tow truck.
