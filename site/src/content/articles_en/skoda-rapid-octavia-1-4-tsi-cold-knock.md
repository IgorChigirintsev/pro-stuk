The Škoda Rapid and Octavia with the turbocharged 1.4 TSI are common cars,
and their owners' complaint is almost word for word the same: “it knocks
for the first seconds after starting, then everything is fine”. Several
components fit that description, and they differ fundamentally in cost —
from a cheap idler to the timing drive. Here is how to tell them apart by
the character of the sound, without dismantling the engine.

## Why a cold start is the moment of truth

With the engine stopped the oil drains into the sump and the system
pressure falls to zero. At start-up every hydraulic element — the chain
tensioner, the hydraulic lifters, the accessory belt tensioner — works for
a second or two without proper support. At the same time the clearances in
cold metal are at their largest, and the oil itself is thicker and pumps
worse.

The result is a natural stress test: a component whose wear is still
within tolerance stays silent on a warm engine but gives itself away at a
cold start. So a cold knock almost always appears before any other
symptom and deserves attention even if it lasts two seconds.

## Three typical sources

| Sound | Duration | What it usually is |
|---|---|---|
| Chattering metallic rattle | 1–2 seconds after starting | Timing chain and its tensioner |
| Rapid dry ticking | 10–60 seconds, fading with warm-up | Hydraulic lifters, oil starvation |
| Squeal or screech turning into a whine | When cold, when load is applied | Accessory belt, idlers, alternator |
| Dull knock when pulling away | Independent of warm-up | Engine mounts |
| Rattle from below | At idle, felt in the body | Heat shield, exhaust mountings |

Chain rattle is the most recognisable: short, chattering and ending
abruptly as soon as oil pressure builds. The full picture of drive wear by
stage is in
[signs of a stretched timing chain](/en/articles/signs-of-a-stretched-timing-chain/)
and
[signs of a failing timing chain tensioner](/en/articles/timing-chain-tensioner-failure-signs/).
Lifter ticking, by contrast, lasts longer and fades gradually —
[hydraulic lifters ticking when cold](/en/articles/hydraulic-lifters-ticking-when-cold/).
And if the sound is closer to a squeal and changes when the air
conditioning comes on or the wheel is turned, the accessories are the
place to look —
[can you drive with a squealing belt](/en/articles/can-you-drive-with-a-squealing-belt/).
The catalogue of cold sounds is on the symptom page
[engine knock when cold](/en/symptoms/engine-knock-when-cold/).

## What is checked first

Diagnosis sensibly runs from cheap to expensive. First, the oil: level,
service life, correct viscosity. Thickened or worn-out oil by itself
produces both ticking and a lengthened chain rattle, and at this stage the
question is sometimes closed by a change.

Second, the accessories. The belt and idlers are checked in minutes, and
their noise is easily taken for something internal: briefly removing the
belt and starting the engine shows whether the cause is still inside.

It is also worth listening for the engine mounts: their wear gives a dull
knock when pulling away and changing gear, unrelated to warm-up but easily
lost in the general noise of a cold start —
[signs of worn engine mounts](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-engine-mounts/).

Third, the camshaft and crankshaft position codes. A correlation fault
points at chain stretch directly, without dismantling. Only after that
does it make sense to remove the cover and look at the tensioner's
position and the state of the guides.

## How the repair is decided

The gap between the cheapest and the dearest outcome here is almost two
orders of magnitude, and that is the whole point of a careful diagnosis:
the cost of misidentifying the source is higher than the cost of the
inspection itself.

Two questions worth asking when the timing drive is quoted. Whether the
complete kit is included — chain, tensioner, guides and sprockets — since
a new chain on worn guides returns the noise quickly. And whether the oil
service history explains the wear: on this engine family stretched
intervals and oil outside the manufacturer's specification are the usual
reason the hydraulic elements are noisy in the first place, and replacing
parts without changing that habit buys a shorter reprieve than the owner
expects.
