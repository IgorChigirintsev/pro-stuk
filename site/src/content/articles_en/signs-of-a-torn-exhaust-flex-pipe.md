A torn flex pipe gives itself away sharply and unambiguously: yesterday
the car sounded normal, today it roars under the driver's feet loudly
enough that you have to raise your voice. The flex pipe is a short
flexible section of the downpipe just behind the engine, woven from metal
mesh over a bellows. Its job is to absorb the movement of an engine that
constantly shifts on its mounts, and to stop that movement destroying the
rigid exhaust system.

## Why the flex pipe fails first

It works in bending and stretching every time the engine rocks: pulling
away, changing gear, on sharp throttle. Its life is finite by definition,
but several things bring the end closer.

Worn engine mounts are the main one: the more the engine moves, the more
travel the flex pipe has to absorb. Next comes corrosion: thin mesh rots
from road salt and condensate faster than thick-walled pipe. Impacts from
below play their part, as does a poor repair where the system is fitted
under tension or, conversely, left sagging on dead rubber hangers.

## What it looks and sounds like

The set of signs is almost always the same:

- **A loud roar around the front of the floor** that rises sharply when
  pulling away and changing gear and quietens slightly at a steady cruise.
  The sound is felt near your feet rather than behind you at the tailpipe.
- **Vibration and rattling at idle**, felt in the floor and the pedals:
  the system has lost its damper and now passes engine movement straight
  into the body. Related sensations are on the symptom page
  [vibration at idle](/en/symptoms/vibration-at-idle/).
- **A smell of exhaust in the cabin**, especially at traffic lights and at
  idle with the ventilation on.
- **Higher fuel consumption** and sometimes a mixture fault code: air is
  drawn in through the tear and the oxygen sensor ahead of the converter
  sees a distorted picture. The same logic at the other end of the engine
  is in
  [signs of an intake air leak](/en/articles/signs-of-an-intake-air-leak/).
- **A visible tear.** From underneath you can see soot and frayed mesh at
  the tear, and with the engine running a pulsing jet of gas comes out of
  it.

A quick check: with the engine cold, start the car and hold a palm near
the flex pipe from the side without touching the metal. An intact section
lets nothing out; a damaged one gives noticeable pulses of air.

## What not to confuse it with

| What you hear | Where to look |
|---|---|
| A roar that appeared suddenly, vibration at idle | The flex pipe, the manifold joint |
| Loudness grew over months, popping on lift-off | A hole in the silencer or a pipe |
| Thin sheet-metal rattle in a narrow rev band | [A heat shield](/en/articles/finding-a-rattling-heat-shield/) |
| The sound follows road speed, not revs | The running gear: [wheel bearing or tyres](/en/articles/hum-wheel-bearing-or-tyres/) |
| Squeaking only when braking | [The brake pad wear indicator](/en/articles/brake-pad-wear-indicator-sound/) |
| A pouring rustle over bumps | [Broken converter ceramic](/en/articles/broken-catalytic-converter-rattle/) |

The key to separating them is what the sound depends on. Everything
related to the exhaust follows engine revs: add throttle and it gets
louder, coast in neutral and it fades. Everything related to the running
gear follows road speed and ignores the throttle. Popping in the exhaust
has its own symptom page —
[exhaust backfire](/en/symptoms/exhaust-backfire/).

## How the repair is decided

The work is straightforward and almost always done in one visit: the old
flex pipe is cut out and a new one welded in, occasionally clamped. While
you are there, ask for the engine mounts and the exhaust hangers to be
checked — if the cause of the engine's extra movement is not fixed, the
new flex pipe will not last long.

Two things worth asking. Whether the replacement is a proper flex section
of the right length and diameter rather than a universal one squeezed in:
a flex pipe fitted under tension tears again within months. And whether
the system will be hung in its correct position while the weld cools —
welded up with the exhaust pulled to one side, the new section starts its
life pre-loaded.

If the roar has appeared but you are not sure whether it is the flex pipe
or a rotten pipe, record the sound in the Stuk app at idle and while
raising the revs smoothly. The app matches the recording with your answers
about where the sound is loudest and how it changes, and suggests which
part of the system to inspect first.
