Engine mounts are the rubber-and-metal assemblies the engine hangs on
inside the body. Their job is twofold: to hold the weight of the unit and
to damp its vibration so it does not reach the cabin. The rubber ages,
cracks and tears, and hydraulic mounts (with fluid inside) lose their
filling — and then the car starts to shake, jolt and knock. Here are the
signs in order, from the first hints to the state where nothing should be
put off.

## The main sign: vibration at idle

The most common and earliest symptom is a fine shake with the car
stationary and the engine running. The steering wheel vibrates, the mirror
trembles, small things rattle in the cup holder. The characteristic
details:

- in neutral the vibration is weaker; in drive with the brake held it is
  noticeably stronger, because the loaded engine leans onto the worn
  mount;
- as revs rise the shake often disappears or changes character;
- it can change with warm-up — rubber stiffened by cold behaves
  differently from warm rubber.

On cars with automatic gearboxes worn mounts show up earlier and more
clearly: sitting in drive on the brake is their standard torture, with the
engine constantly pulling against the brake and pushing on the rear mount.
On a manual the same fault stays unnoticed longer — in neutral in traffic
the engine barely loads the mounts at all.

Similar shaking comes from other causes too, from spark plugs to blocked
injectors, so the full set of lookalikes is on the symptom page
[vibration at idle](/en/symptoms/vibration-at-idle/).

## Jolts and knocks: mounts under load

When the rubber is torn the engine gains freedom of movement, and
mechanical symptoms appear:

- **a jolt when selecting drive or reverse** — a perceptible lurch of the
  body as the transmission loads the engine;
- **a lunge on sharp throttle and lift-off** — the engine rocks back and
  forth, sometimes with an audible dull thump against its stop;
- **a knock under acceleration and braking** — a detached mount lets the
  engine hit its bracket; how to tell that knock from the others is on
  [knocking when accelerating](/en/symptoms/knocking-when-accelerating/);
- **shaking and rattling under the floor** — a rocking engine pulls on the
  exhaust and it starts touching the body; a similar picture is described
  in
  [rattling under the floor at speed](/en/articles/rattling-under-the-floor-at-speed/).

## The stages of mount wear

| Stage | What you notice | How urgent |
|---|---|---|
| Early | Light shake at idle in drive, the wheel vibrates | Watch, check at the next service |
| Middle | Jolts on selecting a gear, small items rattling in the cabin | Diagnosis within weeks |
| Marked | Knock on acceleration/lift-off, the engine visibly rocking | Replace without delay |
| Critical | Thumps at every manoeuvre, stretched hoses, the exhaust touching | Drive only to the workshop |

How fast it degrades depends on the design: an ordinary rubber mount dies
over years; a hydraulic mount that has lost its fluid dies in weeks,
because its rubber was never meant to work without hydraulic support.

## How to check the mounts yourself

You need a helper. Open the bonnet, stand to one side and have the helper
load the engine gently with the brake held: select drive, add a little
throttle, then reverse. Watch the engine: a lean of a couple of
centimetres with a smooth return is normal; a sharp lurch with noticeable
travel or a dull thump means free play in a mount. The full procedure is
in
[checking engine mounts under load](/en/articles/checking-engine-mounts-under-load/).

The second test is inspection. With a torch, look at the accessible
mounts: cracks right through the rubber, rubber separating from metal,
fluid stains on a hydraulic mount — all are reasons for replacement. A
sagged engine is sometimes visible in the clearances too: the pulleys sit
lower than usual, the exhaust is stretched at the manifold joint.

Bear in mind that not every mount is visible from above: the lower torque
link on many front-wheel-drive cars hides behind the subframe and can only
be inspected from a pit or a lift. If the upper mounts look alive but the
symptoms are there, the lower one is very likely torn — and that is the
most common outcome of the diagnosis.

Remember too that dull knocks from below also come from exhaust mountings
and the undertray — before blaming the mounts it is worth ruling out the
simple causes in
[clattering under the car over bumps](/en/articles/clattering-under-the-car-over-bumps/),
where the source is cured by tightening fasteners.

## How the repair is decided

There is no need to replace every mount at once: the ones that pass
inspection will happily see out their life. But checking the whole set
while one is being replaced is sensible — their loads are connected, and
a fresh stiff mount next to three tired ones takes more than its share.

On the choice of part the guide is this: for ordinary driving a quality
aftermarket mount from a known rubber-to-metal manufacturer is enough,
while an original is justified for hydraulic mounts with complex internals.
A very cheap mount made of hard rubber formally lasts a long time but
passes into the body exactly the vibration the mount existed to damp — a
dubious saving.

One thing workshops do not always mention: the bolts. Many mount fixings
are stretch bolts specified for single use and a torque-plus-angle
sequence, and the engine has to be supported at the right height while
they are tightened. A mount bolted up with the engine hanging at the wrong
angle sits pre-loaded and tears again within months.
