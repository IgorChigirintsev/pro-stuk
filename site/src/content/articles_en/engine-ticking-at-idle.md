Ticking at idle is a high, rapid sound, like a sewing machine or a
fingernail tapping on tin. It is almost always born in the upper part of
the engine, where the valves work, and in most cases it does not mean
catastrophe. But ticking has its worrying scenarios too, so it matters to
work out which group yours belongs to: standard noise, a signal to book a
service within the month, or a reason to go for a diagnosis this week.

## Where ticking comes from: four main sources

Practically all ticking at idle comes from four parts:

- **Valve clearances.** A thermal clearance is left by design between the
  cam lobe and the valve. When it grows beyond specification, the lobe
  strikes the valve with a tick. Typical of engines with manual
  adjustment.
- **Hydraulic lifters** — small plungers that take up that clearance
  automatically using oil pressure. A worn or air-filled lifter ticks
  because it no longer holds the clearance.
- **Injectors.** On direct injection engines the injectors open with
  distinct clicks — that is normal, as covered in
  [ticking injectors: normal or not](/en/articles/ticking-injectors-normal-or-not/).
- **The timing chain.** A stretched chain or a weak tensioner gives a
  rustling tick with a metallic edge, often more noticeable on a throttle
  blip.

A fifth, less obvious candidate is the accessories: drive belt idler
bearings and the alternator decoupler pulley chatter at idle very much
like valve ticking. Telling them apart is easy: at a workshop the engine
is run for a few seconds with the drive belt removed — if the sound has
gone, look at the idlers and the alternator rather than inside the engine.

## Telling the sources apart by conditions

| Sign | Likely source | What it means |
|---|---|---|
| Even ticking, there since you bought the car | Injectors | Normal, do nothing |
| Ticks for 3–10 seconds after standing, then quiet | Hydraulic lifters | Watch the oil |
| Constant ticking, rises with revs | Clearances or lifters | Diagnosis within the month |
| Ticking with a rustle, a burst on a throttle blip | Timing chain | Diagnosis this week |
| Ticking plus misfiring, the engine shakes | More than the valve train | Diagnose without delay |

Frequency is an extra clue. Camshafts turn at half crankshaft speed, so
valve ticking sounds at "half" the revs. A sound at the same frequency as
the revs is more often connected with the piston group or the accessories.

Another technique is locating it. A mechanic's stethoscope, or a sheet of
stiff paper rolled into a tube, is held against the cam cover, then the
front cover by the chain, then the alternator body. Where the ticking is
loudest through the tube is the source — a crude method, but it works, and
it is set out in
[a DIY mechanic's stethoscope](/en/articles/diy-mechanics-stethoscope/).

## The harmless cases

Two scenarios need no repair. The first is the injector chatter of direct
injection: identical for years, independent of oil and warm-up, audible
outside and barely audible in the cabin. The second is a short tick from
the hydraulic lifters after long standing: the oil has drained from them
into the sump and for the first seconds they run dry. If the sound goes
within 5–10 seconds, that is acceptable, though it hints that the oil and
filter would not mind refreshing —
[hydraulic lifters ticking when cold](/en/articles/hydraulic-lifters-ticking-when-cold/).

A separate case is the diesel-like rumble of modern engines with variable
valve timing at idle, which manufacturers describe outright as a
characteristic of operation. Comparing with a healthy car of the same
model helps: if your neighbour's engine sounds the same, there is no cause
for concern.

A third scenario is not about the engine at all: relay clicks, the
buzzing of the purge valve, the hum of the fuel pump from under the rear
seat are sometimes taken for engine sounds. The difference is that their
rhythm has nothing to do with engine speed.

## When ticking is a warning

Oil is involved in almost every case in this group. Hydraulic lifters are
the first to react to a dropped level, a blocked filter and lubricant that
has lost its viscosity — they report oil starvation with a tick before the
pressure warning light comes on. So an engine that suddenly starts ticking
is a reason to look at the dipstick the same day, as set out in
[signs of low oil pressure](/en/articles/signs-of-low-oil-pressure/).

Take notice if the ticking has appeared recently and is progressing. Valve
clearances that have drifted eventually lead to a burnt valve — an
expensive cylinder head repair. Lifters knocking on dirty oil hint at oil
starvation, which goes on to hit the bearings and camshafts. A stretched
chain, in the worst case, jumps a tooth — and then the valves meet the
pistons.

Ticking joined by misfires deserves separate attention: the engine shakes,
the idle wanders. That combination is covered in
[an engine that misfires and knocks](/en/articles/engine-misfires-and-knocks/).
And if on a warm engine the ticking turns into a bright metallic sound
under acceleration, that is a different mechanism with different risks —
see
[metallic pinging under acceleration](/en/articles/metallic-pinging-under-acceleration/).
You can compare your sound with typical recordings on the symptom pages
[engine ticking](/en/symptoms/ticking-engine/) and
[vibration at idle](/en/symptoms/vibration-at-idle/).

## How the repair is decided

The order runs from cheap to expensive. First the level and condition of
the oil: if the change was a long time ago, fresh oil with a new filter
often removes lifter ticking entirely — and it is the cheapest step on the
list. Then the clearances: on engines with adjustment it is combined with
scheduled servicing. Replacing the lifters themselves is a larger job,
because on many engines it means removing the camshafts. The most
expensive scenario is a timing chain kit with tensioner and guides.

Two things mechanics do not always say. The labour for lifters and for
valve clearances varies enormously between engine designs — on one it is a
couple of hours, on another half a day with the camshafts out — so ask
which case yours is before comparing quotes. And whatever is replaced, the
oil regime that caused the wear has to change too: fresh parts fed by the
same neglected oil start ticking again within a year.
