The K4M engine was fitted to the Renault Logan, Sandero, Duster, Megane
and related models, and one complaint has followed it for most of its
life: a sharp ticking from under the valve cover on a cold start. The
phrase “the hydraulic lifters are knocking” is usually right about the
address but not always right about the conclusion — because the lifters
themselves are more often the victim than the culprit. Here is the
mechanism and the order of work.

## How it works and why it ticks

A hydraulic lifter is a small plunger cup between the camshaft lobe and
the valve. Engine oil is fed into it under pressure, the plunger extends
and takes up the thermal clearance so that the valve train always works
without free play. While there is oil inside, the clearance is zero and
there is no sound. As soon as the oil escapes or its pressure falls, a gap
opens between the lobe and the follower — and every revolution of the
camshaft becomes an impact, heard as rapid, sharp ticking from the top of
the engine.

Hence a simple rule: a lifter ticks either because it is not receiving oil
of the right quality and pressure, or because it can no longer hold the
oil inside — through wear in the plunger or coked-up passages. The first
case is cured by servicing, the second by replacing parts.

## Normal, or no longer normal

| The pattern of the sound | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| Ticking for 2–5 seconds after starting, then silence | Standard behaviour; the oil has not filled the lifters yet |
| Ticking for up to a minute in the cold, then gone | Borderline: oil viscosity, early-stage wear |
| The noise stays after warm-up | Worn lifters, blocked passages, oil pressure |
| One or two distinct "voices" ticking | Specific lifters or their oil passages |
| A dull knock that grows under load | No longer the valves — look lower down the engine |

The decisive marker is warm-up. Ticking that honestly disappears once the
engine is warm rarely needs immediate intervention. A knock that stays on
a hot engine is worth showing to a mechanic. The sound portraits of this
group and its neighbours are on the symptom page
[engine knock when cold](/en/symptoms/engine-knock-when-cold/).

## What it is not

The first thing to rule out is the timing drive. The K4M uses a belt, so
the characteristic chain rattle at startup should not be present here.
Second, the cam phaser: on engines that have one, a short rumble at
startup comes from it, and the cure is different from valve ticking.
Third, the accessory drive: idlers, tensioner, alternator. Those are
checked by seeing how the sound changes with the accessory belt removed.

The practical order of diagnosis looks like this: check the level and
condition of the oil, recall when it was changed and which viscosity went
in, listen to the engine with a stethoscope at points across the valve
cover, and measure oil pressure if needed. Only after that does it make
sense to talk about removing camshafts.

## How the repair is decided

The order of spending follows the order of likelihood. Oil and filter
first, then two or three cold starts of observation, and only then the
cylinder head. The question is closed at the first step surprisingly
often — particularly if the oil ran longer than it should have, or was
chosen by guesswork rather than by specification.

If the lifters do turn out to be worn, two things are worth agreeing
before the work starts. The whole set is replaced rather than the noisy
one: they have the same age and the same mileage, and doing one means
paying for the same disassembly twice. And the camshaft lobes are
inspected while the engine is open — a lifter that has been ticking for
months leaves marks on the lobe, and finding that out afterwards is the
expensive way.
