An intake air leak is one of the most underrated faults: it almost never
breaks an engine, but it reliably ruins its character. The engine starts
living a life of its own at idle, hesitates on light throttle, starts
badly, uses extra fuel — and a diagnostic scan produces codes that lead in
the wrong direction, for instance to an oxygen sensor that is actually
healthy. Here is where the extra air comes from, how it shows itself, and
why it is hunted with smoke rather than by guesswork.

## Where the extra air comes from

The intake tract from the air filter to the cylinders has to be sealed.
Everything that enters it passes through the mass airflow meter or is
accounted for by a pressure sensor, and from that data the ECU calculates
fuel delivery. If air enters downstream of the measurement, through a gap,
the electronics do not know about it: less fuel than needed is delivered
and the mixture goes lean.

The typical leak points are cracked crankcase ventilation hoses, hardened
intake manifold and throttle body gaskets, injector seals, the brake
servo hose, unions and blanking plugs, and on turbocharged engines the
intercooler joints and clamps. Rubber ages from heat and time, plastic
pipes become brittle and split along their bends.

## How it shows itself

The classic set of signs looks like this:

- **A hunting idle.** The tachometer needle swings up and down by 100–300
  rpm with no input from the driver.
- **A high idle after warm-up** — the engine hangs at 1,100–1,300 instead
  of the usual 750–850.
- **A whistle or hiss under the bonnet** that gets quieter when you add
  throttle: the vacuum is highest at idle, so the sound is loudest there.
  Similar under-bonnet sounds at startup are collected on the symptom page
  [squeal on startup](/en/symptoms/squeal-on-startup/).
- **Hesitation on light throttle and flat spots when pulling away.**
- **Positive fuel trims** on the scan tool — the ECU is constantly adding
  fuel to compensate for unmeasured air.
- **Unstable cold starting**, while on a warm engine everything is nearly
  normal.

Shaking at idle has other causes too — the full set is on the symptom page
[vibration at idle](/en/symptoms/vibration-at-idle/), and misfires with
knocking in
[the engine misfires and knocks](/en/articles/engine-misfires-and-knocks/).

## How leaks are found

| Method | What it shows | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Smoke machine | Smoke comes straight out of the gap — the most graphic method | Needs a workshop with the equipment |
| Fuel trims on a scan tool | Confirms a lean mixture and how it varies with revs | Does not give the exact location |
| Spraying with cleaner | The revs change when it hits a leaking joint | Fire risk, needs care |
| Inspecting and squeezing hoses by hand | Finds obvious cracks and slipped clamps | Misses hidden gaskets |
| Checking when cold | The gap is at its largest, the sound clearer | You have to catch it before warm-up |

The smoke machine remains the main tool: the tract is filled with thick
harmless smoke at slight pressure, and it reveals a leak where none can be
heard. The DIY methods are legitimate as a preliminary check, but spraying
flammable liquids on a hot engine is a risky business and should not be
overused.

A separate piece of advice: before replacing sensors on a fault code, rule
out an air leak. A lean mixture and “wrong” oxygen sensor readings are a
common pair where the culprit is not the sensor but a perished hose.

## How the repair is decided

The cost of the fix varies enormously: replacing a split crankcase
ventilation hose is minutes of work, while an intake manifold gasket
requires stripping the top of the engine. The sensible order is to
localise the leak precisely first and only then discuss the scope of work.

Two things worth knowing. First, plastic intake components on many modern
engines are supplied only as a complete manifold, so “replace the gasket”
can turn into a much larger part — ask before agreeing. Second, when a
leak has been running for a while, the throttle body and the idle control
passages are usually sooted up, and cleaning them at the same time is what
stops the idle hunting after the leak itself is cured.
