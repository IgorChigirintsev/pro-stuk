A rattling dashboard rarely means a fault, but it reliably spoils how a
car feels: silence on smooth tarmac, and then a fine crackling somewhere
in the dash at the first joint in the concrete. What makes it irritating
is how elusive it seems — stop the car, press the plastic with your hand,
and it is gone. Here is where the rattle comes from, how to find the
contact point in a single drive, and when the dashboard is not the
culprit at all.

## Why plastic starts to buzz

A dashboard is not one part but a dozen trim pieces, air ducts and
brackets assembled on clips and screws. Manufacturing gaps are designed
in between them: plastic expands in heat and contracts in cold, and
without those gaps it would simply buckle. While the joints are held by
seals and felt pads, the parts move against each other silently.

Over time the felt wears away, the clips lose their grip, and the plastic
itself distorts slightly after years of temperature swings. A free
movement of fractions of a millimetre appears — and at the right
vibration frequency two parts start tapping against each other hundreds
of times a second. Hence the characteristic pattern: the rattle does not
turn up on every bump but on a particular surface, or at particular
engine speeds, where the resonance lines up.

## Finding the contact point

The search rests on a single trick: load the suspect area with your hand
while driving. If the sound disappears, you have found the source.

1. **Establish the conditions.** Fine washboard tarmac, a large expansion
   joint, idling at a traffic light — each variant points to its own
   frequency and narrows the field.
2. **Take a passenger.** It is awkward and unsafe for a driver to feel
   around the dash while moving; a passenger can press each panel calmly
   in turn.
3. **Press by sector.** The joint between the windscreen and the dash
   top, the side vents, the glovebox lid, the trim around the stereo, the
   pillars by the doors.
4. **Check the contents.** Coins in a cubby, glasses on the dash top, a
   dash camera on its sucker and its cable produce up to half of all
   interior rattles.

The same method works for noises in the doors and trim panels.

## Where the source usually is

| Place | How it shows | What fixes it |
|---|---|---|
| Dash top against the windscreen | Sharp crackling on small bumps | A felt or anti-squeak tape shim |
| Glovebox lid | Dull knock on large joints | Tightening the hinges, a seal around the edge |
| Air vents | Fine buzzing at idle | Fixing the vanes, tightening the housing |
| Stereo surround | The noise follows the bass in the speakers | A seal around the frame |
| Instrument cluster | Rattle while warming up, then quiet | Tightening the mounts with the cluster out |
| Wiring behind the dash | Random knocking, no link to the surface | Cable ties, fixing the loom to the body |

The universal remedy is anti-squeak material: thin self-adhesive felt
glued into the gap. It does not remove the free movement, but it makes
the contact soft, and the impact becomes a silent touch.

## When it is not the dashboard

Sometimes the plastic is only the resonator and the cause lies deeper. If
the rattle appears strictly at certain engine speeds and does not follow
the road, check the engine mounts: a worn mount passes vibration into the
body, and the dashboard is the first thing to answer. If the noise comes
from below and appears over bumps, the chassis is the more likely
suspect. To tell an interior rattle from one underneath the car, compare
your case with the description on the symptom page
[knocking over bumps](/en/symptoms/knocking-over-bumps/).

There is a separate group of sounds that only seem to come from the
cabin. Gearbox whine and water pump hum travel through the bulkhead
easily and are perceived as noise from “somewhere under the dash”.

One practical note before booking any work: interior rattles are billed
by time, not by part, and the search is the expensive half. Arriving with
a description of the exact conditions — which surface, which speed,
whether it depends on temperature — is what keeps that search short. If
you have already found the panel that silences the noise when pressed,
say so first.
