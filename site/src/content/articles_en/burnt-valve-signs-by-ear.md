A burnt valve is a burnt-away section on the seating face of the valve
head, through which the cylinder loses its seal. After that the engine
cannot compress the mixture properly in one cylinder, and the car acquires
a set of noticeable symptoms, from a rough idle to characteristic popping
in the exhaust. The theory can be formed fairly confidently by ear, but it
still has to be confirmed with instruments. Here is both.

## What it sounds like

The main sign is **rhythmic popping in the exhaust** at a frequency
related to engine speed. A muffled “puff-puff” with an even interval comes
from the tailpipe; it is clearer at idle, and with the throttle blipped
the frequency rises with the revs. If an intake valve has burnt, the
popping goes into the intake instead: a sharp cough around the air filter
or the throttle body.

The second layer is **a change in how the engine runs**. Misfiring
appears, the idle starts hunting, vibration is felt in the wheel and the
floor, and the car loses power. A telling detail: the symptom is stable
and does not depend on warm-up, unlike many ignition problems.

The third is **hissing or whistling** at low revs: air passes through the
imperfect valve seat. The catalogue of popping and banging in the exhaust
with its causes is on the symptom page
[exhaust backfire](/en/symptoms/exhaust-backfire/).

## Telling it from similar cases

| Cause | Character of the sound | Key difference |
|---|---|---|
| Burnt valve | Rhythmic popping, misfiring | Low compression in the cylinder, not cured by plugs |
| Ignition misfire | Similar popping, jerking | Moves when the coil or plug is swapped |
| Manifold gasket | Ticking, hissing at the engine | Louder when cold, compression normal |
| Rich mixture | Banging in the exhaust on lift-off | Black smoke, a smell of fuel |
| A failed converter | Rattles, reduces power | The sound is from the canister, not the head |

The most common confusion is with a burnt exhaust manifold gasket: its
ticking is also rhythmic and also follows the revs. Misfiring combined
with knocking has its own breakdown in
[the engine misfires and knocks](/en/articles/engine-misfires-and-knocks/),
and metallic pinging under load in
[signs of engine detonation](/en/articles/signs-of-engine-detonation/).

## What confirms the theory

Hearing gives the direction, instruments the proof.

1. **Reading the codes.** A misfire code for a specific cylinder narrows
   the search, though it does not name the cause by itself.
2. **Swapping the plug and coil** into a neighbouring cylinder. If the
   problem moves, the ignition is to blame. If it stays, it is mechanical.
3. **A compression test.** The problem cylinder reads noticeably lower
   than the rest.
4. **A leak-down test.** Air is fed into the cylinder: hissing in the
   exhaust means the exhaust valve, in the intake means the intake valve,
   in the cooling system means the head gasket.
5. **An endoscope.** Inspecting the valve head and the piston crown
   through the plug hole settles it without disassembly.

Separately: if a foreign sound is only audible on the move and linked to
steering, the engine is not involved at all — those cases are covered in
[signs of a worn outer CV joint](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-outer-cv-joint/).

## How the repair is decided

Two things decide it, and neither is the noise itself.

The first is the cause, because it decides whether the story repeats. A
burnt valve usually grows out of incorrect valve clearances: the valve
stops seating fully, loses the contact through which it sheds heat, and
gradually burns away. Overheating, detonation on poor fuel, faulty
injectors and driving with long-standing misfires all help it along —
backfiring into the intake is a related warning, covered in
[intake backfire: the causes](/en/articles/intake-backfire-causes/). After a
head repair it is sensible to check what led to it, or the new valve
follows the same path.

The second is the scope. A head repair means a new valve and seat work,
but the question worth asking is what the endoscope showed on the piston
and the bore: burnt valve material can score both, and that is the
difference between a head job and a rebuild. Ask for that inspection
before the head comes off, not after the parts are ordered.

The difference between those two answers is the price of delay. Driving
with rhythmic popping and misfiring leads directly to an overheated
converter and a damaged cylinder.
