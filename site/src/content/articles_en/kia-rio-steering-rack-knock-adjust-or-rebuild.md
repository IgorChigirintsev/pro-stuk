A chattering knock from somewhere around your feet that appears on small
bumps and is especially clear at low speed is one of the most common
worries for Kia Rio owners. In most cases the source really is the
steering, but not always the rack — and when it is the rack, it does not
always need replacing. Here is where the sound comes from, how to confirm
the address and what workshops actually choose.

## Where a rack knock comes from

A steering rack is a rack and pinion: a gear on the steering shaft and a
toothed bar that moves across the car. A spring-loaded yoke with an
adjuster nut presses the bar against the gear. A car spends most of its
life going almost straight, so the teeth wear unevenly — most of all in
the central zone around the straight-ahead. When the wear exceeds the
yoke's compensating travel, a clearance appears between the teeth. Every
impact from the road passes through the wheels, the rods and the bar, the
clearance works — and a dry click arrives in the cabin.

On compact cars the effect is more audible because of the short firm
suspension and small cabin: the sound barely has room to dissipate. Broken
roads, washboard surfaces and the habit of crossing speed humps at an
angle all add to it.

## Rack or not the rack

Before dismantling any steering, rule out the neighbours — they knock
similarly and cost several times less.

| Source | Character of the sound | How it is checked |
|---|---|---|
| Rack and pinion | Dry click from the centre, on fine shaking | A hand on the rack housing while the wheel is rocked |
| Anti-roll bar links | Rattle over bumps, ignores the steering | Rocking the raised suspension |
| Track rod ends | Knock when the wheel is rocked while parked | Play check on a lift |
| Strut top mount bearings | Crunch and clicks when steering while parked | A hand on the spring while the wheel turns |
| Rack mounting bushes | Dull thump, the sound wanders | Checking the clamp fasteners |

A simple home test: with the car parked and the engine off, jerk the wheel
a couple of centimetres left and right a few times while keeping a hand on
the rack housing under the dashboard. A knock synchronous with your
movements points the suspicion at the steering. If the sound only arrives
on the move over bumps and the steering has nothing to do with it, start
with the dampers and the anti-roll bar links — their full picture is in
[signs of worn shock absorbers](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-shock-absorbers/),
and the general catalogue of similar sounds is on the symptom page
[knocking in the suspension](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-suspension/). Worn
steering ends have their own portrait in
[signs of worn track rod ends](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-track-rod-ends/).

## Adjust or rebuild

An adjustment means turning the yoke's adjuster nut to remove the
clearance in the rack and pinion. The procedure is quick and cheap, and
with moderate wear it honestly solves the problem. There are two limits.
First, the adjustment only works while the yoke has travel left — on a
badly worn rack there is nowhere left to turn. Second, an over-adjusted
rack becomes heavy, the wheel stops self-centring, and that is noticeably
worse than a knock.

A rebuild with dismantling means new bushes, seals, the yoke itself, and
regrinding or replacing the bar. It is chosen when the knock is joined by
fluid stains on the bellows, heavy or binding steering, or unequal effort
in different positions. Replacing the complete rack is the last resort,
usually with heavy wear or a damaged housing — the full symptom list is in
[signs of a faulty steering rack](/en/articles/steering-rack-fault-signs/).

## Urgency and what to ask

You can drive with a rack knock for weeks, and there is no need to panic.
But postponing the diagnosis is only reasonable while the sound is even
and unchanging. Play in the wheel, fluid stains, a change in effort or
foreign grinding when turning mean going to a workshop within days.

Two things worth asking there. Whether the adjustment has any travel left
before it is done — an adjuster already at its limit means the answer is a
rebuild, not a tweak. And whether an alignment follows: any work involving
the tie rods changes the toe.
