A car makes dozens of noises, and nearly all of them mean the same thing:
book a workshop visit this week. But a small group of signals works
differently — with them every kilometre either multiplies the repair bill
or is already a matter of safety. Here are ten such noises, sorted by how
quickly the consequences arrive, with an explanation of what exactly is
breaking in each case.

## How the list was drawn up

The ten share three properties: they mean destruction that has already
started rather than wear; they develop fast — over kilometres and hours,
not months; and their consequences are out of all proportion to the cost
of the part itself. Squeaks, rustling, rattles and most hums are not on
the list: they are unpleasant, but they give you thousands of kilometres
of margin.

## The ten

| № | Noise | What is happening | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Dull knock in time with revs, louder under load | Crankshaft bearing wear | Stop the engine, recovery truck |
| 2 | Any knock together with the oil pressure light | Oil starvation | Stop the engine immediately |
| 3 | Brake pedal sinking, hissing | A circuit has lost pressure | Stop, recovery truck |
| 4 | Metal grinding on metal when braking | Pads worn out completely | To a workshop at low speed |
| 5 | Clattering and dragging under the floor | Undertray or exhaust part detached | Stop and inspect |
| 6 | A sharp bang with the car pulling aside | Tyre failure | Stop smoothly |
| 7 | Suspension knock with the steering pulling | Ball joint or top mount destroyed | Do not drive it there |
| 8 | Belt squeal turned into a howl plus a burning smell | An accessory unit seizing | Stop the engine, check |
| 9 | A rumble instead of a hum plus vibration in the body | Wheel bearing cage breaking up | To a workshop, no motorways |
| 10 | Grinding and impacts from the gearbox when shifting | Synchroniser or bearing failure | Stop driving |

The first three do not tolerate even a short hop. From the fourth to the
tenth it is usually possible to reach the nearest workshop at up to 40
km/h, but only after an inspection and without going onto a motorway. The
individual portraits are in
[signs of big end bearing knock](/en/articles/signs-of-big-end-bearing-knock/),
[signs of low oil pressure](/en/articles/signs-of-low-oil-pressure/),
[can you drive with grinding brakes](/en/articles/can-you-drive-with-grinding-brakes/),
[signs of a worn ball joint](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-ball-joint/) and
[signs of a worn wheel bearing](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-wheel-bearing/);
the shorter version of this list is in
[noises that mean stop driving now](/en/articles/noises-that-mean-stop-driving-now/).

## What is most often confused

Half of all "dangerous noise" visits end with a harmless diagnosis, and
that is fine — better to check. The typical substitutions are these.
Grinding when braking is confused with squealing from rust on the discs
after rain; the differences and the limits of normal are described on the
symptom page
[grinding when braking](/en/symptoms/grinding-when-braking/) and in
[brake squeal after rain](/en/articles/brake-squeal-after-rain/).
Suspension knock over bumps is confused with the knock of badly secured
luggage in the boot. And interior rattles are regularly taken for a
running gear problem: plastic amplifies vibration beautifully. How to tell
one from the other is covered in
[interior creaking over bumps](/en/articles/interior-creaking-over-bumps/),
[suspension creaking over bumps](/en/articles/suspension-creaking-over-bumps/)
and
[interior squeaks: how to find the source](/en/articles/finding-interior-rattles-and-squeaks/).

The practical rule: if the noise depends on the road and disappears on
smooth tarmac, it is the suspension or the interior, and it can wait. If
the noise depends on engine speed or on pressing the brake, the category
is more serious.

## What delay costs

In nearly every line of that table the difference is severalfold, and it
is exactly the price of one extra journey. Pads caught in time are a set
of pads; the same pads a fortnight later add machined or new discs. A ball
joint found on the lift is one part plus an alignment; the same joint
failing on the move is a wing, a tyre and a tow. A knock from the bottom
end taken to a workshop on a truck is a bearing job; the same knock driven
there is a crankshaft regrind or a rebuild.

Two things worth knowing before deciding to press on. A recovery truck
costs a fraction of any of those escalations — the arithmetic almost never
favours "just making it home". And a noise that fades is not a noise that
has gone: several items on this list quieten as speed, temperature or
steering angle changes, which is precisely how they get ignored until the
expensive stage.
