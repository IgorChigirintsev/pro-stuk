Replacing a belt ought to close the subject of squealing, but cars often
come back from the workshop and squeal again a day or two later — and
sometimes right on the way out. That is not always poor work: the causes
fall into three groups — the new belt itself, installation errors, and the
parts that were the real source of the sound before the change. Here they
are in order.

## What counts as normal

A new multi-rib belt does need a little time for its ribs to bed into the
pulleys: a light rustle over the first few dozen kilometres is within
normal. But a bright squeal never is. A healthy drive with a correctly
fitted fresh belt is silent both cold and with the air conditioning on. If
what you hear is a squeal — how it sounds and what it is confused with is
described on the symptom page
[belt squeal](/en/symptoms/belt-squeal/) — the cause needs finding, not
waiting out.

## Installation errors — the commonest cause

| Error | How it shows itself | The fix |
|---|---|---|
| Insufficient tension | Squeal under load: starting, a/c, steering | Tension to the maker's figure |
| Over-tensioning | Hum and whine, rapid accessory bearing wear | Slacken to the correct figure |
| Belt not seated in the grooves | Squeal immediately after the change, belt offset on a pulley | Refit the belt |
| Wrong size or profile | Tensioner at the end of its travel, belt slack or straining | Select the part by catalogue number |
| Pulleys not cleaned of oil | The squeal returns after a day or two | Fix the leak, clean the pulleys, replace the belt |
| Tired tensioner left in place | Intermittent squeal, tensioner pulley flutters when running | Replace the tensioner |

Two rows deserve explanation. The first is tension set by eye: on cars
with manual adjustment a mechanic without a gauge easily errs in both
directions, and an automatic tensioner with a weak spring will not produce
proper force even with a perfect belt. The second is oil contamination: if
what killed the old belt was oil or coolant weeping, a new belt on dirty
pulleys repeats its fate within days.

## When the part itself is at fault

Less often, but it happens, everything is fitted correctly and it is the
new belt that squeals. Usually that means cheap unbranded parts: a hard
rubber compound does not follow the pulley well, the ribs are moulded with
geometry faults, and the belt is noisy from day one. Branded EPDM belts
have practically none of that problem. The other version is a part of the
wrong length or with a different number of ribs: formally the belt fits,
but the tensioner ends up at the edge of its working range. It is checked
by comparing the catalogue number against the VIN — faster and cheaper
than trying other versions.

## If the belt was never the problem

The most galling scenario: it was not the belt squealing at all, and the
replacement could never have helped. Similar sounds come from alternator
bearings, guide idlers, the water pump and the decoupler pulley — the
one-way clutch on the alternator pulley that smooths jerks in the belt;
when worn it makes noise and rattles. By ear these sources are
distinguishable: belt squeal is intermittent and bright, bearings give a
steady whine that rises with revs. The individual portraits are in
[signs of a worn alternator bearing](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-alternator-bearing/),
[signs of a worn idler pulley](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-idler-pulley/)
and
[alternator decoupler pulley failure signs](/en/articles/alternator-decoupler-pulley-failure-signs/).
If the sound is more of a hum and there are traces of coolant near the
water pump pulley, start with
[a whining water pump: how to check it](/en/articles/water-pump-whine-how-to-check/).

## Finding the cause in ten minutes

1. **Note when it squeals.** Only cold, for the first seconds — tension at
   its limit or moisture; under load (a/c, steering) — under-tensioned;
   constantly — misalignment, size, or another part. The morning scenario
   is covered on
   [squeal on startup](/en/symptoms/squeal-on-startup/) and in
   [squealing under the bonnet when cold](/en/articles/squealing-under-the-bonnet-when-cold/).
2. **Inspect how the belt sits.** With a torch, go round every pulley: the
   belt must lie in all the grooves of each pulley, not shifted onto an
   outer rib.
3. **Judge the tension.** With manual adjustment, roughly 5–10 millimetres
   of deflection under finger pressure on the long run; an automatic
   tensioner has range marks, and the pointer should not sit at the edge.
4. **Turn the idlers by hand.** With the engine off and the belt removed
   or slackened, each idler and pulley should turn smoothly, without
   roughness, play or rustling.
5. **Check the pulleys are clean.** An oily film in the grooves can be
   felt with a finger — and explains a returning squeal better than any
   theory.
6. **Compare with the old belt.** If it has not been thrown away, hold the
   two together: a difference in length of more than a centimetre or a
   different number of ribs is a ready answer.

One more useful guide is where exactly the sound lives. Squeal from
slipping spreads around the whole engine bay, while an idler or bearing
whine is local: a mechanic's stethoscope, or a long screwdriver held
against a stationary part of the housing with due care, finds the source
in a couple of minutes.

## How the repair is decided

If a workshop did the change, start by going back to them: putting right
an under-tensioned or misaligned belt is a normal warranty case, and
decent workshops close it free. The line between warranty and new work is
exactly the line between installation errors and a different part — which
is why locating the source before the conversation is worth ten minutes.

Two things mechanics do not always say. The tensioner and the idlers are
consumables with a service life comparable to the belt's, and replacing
them at the same time costs almost nothing extra in labour because the
belt is already off — a quote that includes only the belt is a quote that
comes back. And if the belt was contaminated, the leak has to be found
first: a new belt fitted over an unfixed leak is money spent twice.
