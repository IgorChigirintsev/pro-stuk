You turn the key and instead of confident cranking there is a dry chatter
or a series of clicks from under the bonnet, and the crankshaft does not
move. It sounds like a dead starter, but in most cases the supply is to
blame: the battery, the terminals or the earth. Here is the starting chain
in order — from cheap and simple to complex — with the signs gathered into
a table you can use to guess the cause in a minute without opening your
wallet.

## Where the chatter comes from

On the starter body sits a solenoid — an electromagnet that does two
things at once: it pushes the pinion into mesh with the flywheel and
closes the heavy contacts, sending a large current to the starter motor.
The solenoid can only pull in confidently at normal voltage.

If there is not enough current, a cycle begins: the solenoid pulls in, the
voltage sags under load, the solenoid drops out, the voltage recovers, the
solenoid pulls in again. Repeated several times a second, that cycle
sounds like machine-gun chatter. So chattering is almost always a symptom
of weak current rather than a broken mechanism: the starter itself may be
perfectly healthy.

## Cause one: battery and contacts

In workshop experience most cases hide here:

- **a flat or tired battery** — after a frosty night, short trips,
  forgotten lights, or simply age; in the cold the capacity falls and
  there is not enough cranking current;
- **corroded terminals** — a whitish or greenish deposit on the posts
  works as resistance, choking the current at the very start;
- **a poor earth** — a corroded or loose strap between the body, the
  engine and the battery: the voltage at the starter sags although the
  battery is healthy.

A quick check: switch on the headlights and turn the key. If the lights
dim noticeably as it chatters, the battery or terminals are the cause; if
they stay bright and the starter still chatters, look further, in the
wiring and the starter itself.

## When the starter itself is to blame

If the supply is sound, the suspects are:

- **burnt heavy contacts in the solenoid** — it clicks but no current
  reaches the motor;
- **worn brushes or bushes** — the starter receives current but cannot
  turn the shaft;
- **the pinion drive** — if the starter whirrs while the engine does not
  turn, the one-way clutch is slipping;
- **a chewed flywheel ring gear** — the pinion hits the teeth and cannot
  engage, and metallic grinding joins the chatter, as covered in
  [grinding noise when starting the engine](/en/articles/grinding-noise-when-starting-the-engine/).

The good news: a starter is a repairable unit. The solenoid, the brushes
and the pinion drive are replaced individually, and that is noticeably
cheaper than a complete starter.

## Sign, cause, action

| Sign | Likely cause | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Chatter, lights dim as you turn the key | Flat battery, terminals | Charge, clean the terminals |
| A single click and silence | Solenoid, contacts, earth | Check the earth and the solenoid |
| Chatter with the lights staying bright | Wiring to the starter, brushes | Electrical diagnosis |
| Chatter with metallic grinding | Pinion drive, ring gear | Workshop, without delay |

A single click with no chatter has its own breakdown in
[the starter clicks but will not crank](/en/articles/starter-clicks-but-wont-crank/).

## What you can do on the spot

The sequence that works most often: inspect and tighten the terminals and
clean off any deposit; try a jump start from another car, observing
polarity; and after a successful start check the battery's charge and the
alternator's output, or the story repeats the next morning.

If the start still fails, the workshop remains. Two things are worth
asking there. Whether the voltage was measured at the starter itself
during cranking rather than only at the battery — that single measurement
separates “weak battery” from “lost current on the way”. And whether the
starter will be rebuilt or replaced: solenoid contacts and brushes are
cheap, and on many starters that is the whole repair.

After winning the start, listen to the engine: a dry rattle in the first
seconds is a different story, covered in
[signs of a failing VVT actuator](/en/articles/vvt-actuator-failure-signs/).
A squeal that appears after starting is a reason to look at the symptom
page [squeal on startup](/en/symptoms/squeal-on-startup/) — that is belt
territory rather than the starter.
