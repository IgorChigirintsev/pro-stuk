The car comes back from a tyre change — and a knock has appeared that was
not there in the morning. A familiar story, especially in the seasonal
changeover. Most often the cause really is wheel-related and is fixed in
minutes, but sometimes the tyre change merely coincides with suspension
wear or makes it audible. Here are the causes from common to rare,
starting with the one that must be ruled out immediately.

## First: the wheel bolts

Under-tightened fasteners are the most common and simultaneously the most
dangerous cause of a knock after a wheel change. It sounds like a metallic
knock or clicks when pulling away, braking and over bumps; sometimes
steering wheel vibration joins in. There is nothing to wait for: stop and
check the torque on all the wheels — with the brace from the car's tool
kit or at the nearest tyre shop.

Correct tightening is done with a torque wrench: for most cars that is
around 100–130 Nm, with the exact figure in the handbook. The check
re-torque after 50–100 km matters too: the wheel beds onto the hub and the
torque can drop even after an honest tightening. Driving on loose bolts
damages the rim's tapered holes and the studs, and in the extreme case ends
with a lost wheel — which is why that check always comes first.

The opposite mistake happens too: over-tightening with an impact gun
stretches the studs and warps the brake disc. It does not usually knock,
but if judder under braking appeared after a tyre change, remember whether
the wheels were done with an impact gun and no torque wrench.

## The common causes

| Cause | How it sounds | How to check |
|---|---|---|
| Loose bolts or nuts | Metallic knock when pulling away, braking, over bumps | Re-torque all the wheels |
| A stone or screw in the tread | Rhythmic clicking, frequency rising with speed | Inspect the tread |
| A stone between rim and caliper | Grinding, ringing, rattling from one wheel | Look at the inner face of the rim |
| A weight or debris under a trim cover | Random rattling, something rolling | Remove the cover |
| A lost balance weight | Not a knock but steering vibration at 80–120 km/h | Rebalance |
| A bent rim, a bulge in a tyre | Judder, hum-vibration at speed | Check on the balancing machine |
| Wrong fasteners or spacers | Knock, judder, fasteners self-loosening | Compare bolts and centring rings with the specification |
| Suspension wear (coincidence) | Dull knock over bumps, present before too | Running gear diagnosis |

Fasteners and centring deserve their own mention. If the wheels were
changed along with the rims, a knock and vibration can come from the wrong
fasteners: bolts with a different seat, missing centring rings, locking
bolts not fully tightened. Check that the same day.

## Knock, vibration or hum are different complaints

Words matter, because they lead to different culprits:

- **a knock** — short impacts over bumps or during manoeuvres: fasteners,
  objects in the wheel, suspension;
- **vibration** — shaking of the wheel or body in a narrow speed band:
  balancing, rim geometry, a tyre bulge;
- **a hum** — an even sound growing with speed: tread pattern and wear,
  less often a wheel bearing.

If a hum specifically appeared after the changeover, the new set is the
first suspect — a different pattern and a different compound sound
different. How to separate tyres from a bearing in one drive is in
[humming: wheel bearing or tyres](/en/articles/hum-wheel-bearing-or-tyres/),
and the full list of bearing signs in
[signs of a worn wheel bearing](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-wheel-bearing/).

## What to check yourself in 15 minutes

1. **Re-torque.** All bolts on all wheels, crosswise. No brace — any tyre
   shop does it in minutes and usually free.
2. **Inspect the tread.** Stones in the grooves, screws, nail heads. Prise
   the stone out and the clicking stops at once.
3. **Trim covers.** Take them off and shake them out: a detached weight, a
   pebble or a forgotten nut rattles there.
4. **A look behind the rim.** Stones between the rim and the caliper, or
   an inner arch liner caught during fitting — a torch and a couple of
   minutes are enough.
5. **A control drive.** The same route: when does it knock — over small
   bumps, under braking, in corners? That link narrows the search sharply
   and is useful to the mechanic.

One more thing to check after a changeover is the boot. An unsecured
spare, the jack or a bag of removed fasteners knock dully over bumps and
convincingly imitate “a knock in the rear suspension”. It sounds like a
joke, but such finds turn up regularly.

## When the tyre shop is not to blame

Lifting the car and swapping wheels is the moment the suspension is heard
anew. A new set is quieter than a worn one, and against that quiet a knock
that existed for a long time becomes noticeable. The classic culprits of a
dull knock over small bumps are the anti-roll bar links, covered in
[signs of worn anti-roll bar links](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-anti-roll-bar-links/).
The bushes and tired dampers knock similarly.
