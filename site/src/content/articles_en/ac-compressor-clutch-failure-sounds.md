The electromagnetic clutch is the part that makes it possible to switch
the air conditioning off at all. The compressor pulley is turned by the
accessory belt all the time, but it only connects to the compressor shaft
when the coil is energised and the pressure plate is pulled against the
pulley. Hence the familiar click under the bonnet when you press the
button. And hence the main diagnostic clue: if a foreign noise appears
exactly at that click and disappears when you switch off, the circle of
suspects narrows sharply.

## How the unit works and what wears out

There are three key elements in the clutch: the pulley with its bearing,
which turns constantly; the electromagnet coil, fixed to the compressor
body; and the pressure plate on the shaft, pulled towards the pulley when
energised. Between the plate and the pulley the factory sets a small air
gap — fractions of a millimetre.

Usually one of three things wears. The pulley bearing, which lives in
engine bay heat under constant belt load. The friction faces of the plate
and the pulley, which wear with every engagement until the gap grows so
far that the coil no longer has the force to pull the plate in. And the
coil itself, which can go open circuit or short between turns.

## The sound portraits

| Sound | When it is heard | Most likely cause |
|---|---|---|
| Even whine or rustle, ignores the button | Always, grows with revs | Clutch pulley bearing |
| Chattering and rattling right after engaging | Only with the a/c on | Worn friction faces, large air gap |
| Metallic grinding with a burning smell | On engaging, the belt squeals | The plate slipping on the pulley |
| Dull knock in time with the revs | With the a/c on | Scoring inside the compressor itself |
| Frequent clicking on and off | In the heat, at idle | Low refrigerant, the pressure switch cycling |
| Belt squeal when engaging | The first seconds after the button | A stretched or oily belt, weak tensioner |

The simple check takes a minute. Start the engine with the a/c off and
listen to the engine bay. Then switch the a/c on and compare. A sound that
appeared exactly with the click is the clutch or the compressor. A sound
that was there before you pressed the button is the pulley bearing or
another accessory idler. The general picture of belt drive noises is on
the symptom page [belt squeal](/en/symptoms/belt-squeal/), and separating
the units behind it is covered in
[whine under the bonnet: alternator or water pump](/en/articles/alternator-or-water-pump-whine/).

## Why it should not be ignored

Clutch noise is unpleasant not in itself but because it sits on the same
belt as everything else. A seized pulley bearing snaps or throws off the
accessory belt, and with it the car loses the alternator, the water pump
and the power steering — that is no longer “inconvenient” but a stop on
the road with a risk of overheating.

The second scenario is slipping. When the gap has grown, the plate is
pulled in loosely and burns; in bad cases the friction faces weld
together, the clutch stops disengaging at all, and the compressor turns
permanently.

Do not confuse the chattering with a noise inside the engine. A sound
coming from the cylinder block that ignores the a/c button is a different
story altogether — compare it with the symptom page
[knocking in the engine](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-engine/) or with
[humming under the bonnet at idle](/en/articles/humming-under-the-bonnet-at-idle/).

## How the repair is decided

The diagnostic order is usually this: inspect and listen to the
accessories, check the system pressures, measure the clutch air gap with a
feeler gauge, check the coil resistance. Some faults are solved without
replacing the compressor: on many cars the clutch is available as a
separate assembly, and sometimes adjusting the gap by removing one shim is
enough.

Two questions are worth asking before you agree to a compressor. First,
what the gap measured — a number, not “it looked worn”. Second, whether
the refrigerant charge and pressures were checked first: a system running
low makes the clutch cycle constantly, and that cycling is what burns the
friction faces. Replacing a compressor without finding the leak means the
new one works under the same conditions that killed the old one.
