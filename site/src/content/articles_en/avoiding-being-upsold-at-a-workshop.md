Most unpleasant workshop stories begin the same way: the owner arrives
with one noise and leaves with a list of six items, half of which they do
not understand. You do not need to know how a car is built to protect
yourself — it is enough to know what a normal diagnostic process looks
like and which deviations from it should make you pause. Below is a
practical set of checks that works the same in an expensive dealership and
in a small independent garage.

## What an honest diagnosis looks like

The inspection starts with a conversation, not a lift. The mechanic asks
when the noise appeared, at what speed it is audible, whether it depends
on steering, braking or warming up. That is not a formality: the
conditions narrow the list of suspects more than a visual inspection does.
Then the car goes up and the joints are checked for play with a pry bar
rather than “by eye”. A good sign is being invited under the car and shown
the specific play by hand.

The second marker is a verdict split into “replace now” and “keep an eye
on it”. Wear is almost always gradual, and an honest mechanic will tell
you that a bush is weeping but will last another season. A list where all
six items are red is statistically unlikely.

The third marker is that the answer relates to your complaint. If you came
in with a hum and the report only mentions suspension, with nothing about
whether the hum is a bearing or the tyres, then nobody addressed the
original question. How those two are separated is covered in
[humming: wheel bearing or tyres](/en/articles/hum-wheel-bearing-or-tyres/).

## Phrases worth a follow-up question

| What the mechanic says | What to ask |
|---|---|
| “Everything is knocking here, the whole suspension needs replacing” | Which specific part produces your noise |
| “The engine is finished, you can hear it in the knock” | Whether a scan and measurements were done |
| “Only genuine parts, only on order” | Whether there are aftermarket options and what they cost |
| “We have already taken it apart, we have to finish” | Why this was not visible before disassembly |
| “The warranty applies only if you replace everything at once” | What exactly the warranty covers |

None of those phrases automatically means dishonesty. The point is that a
conscientious mechanic always has a calm answer to a follow-up question,
while a dishonest one starts applying pressure about urgency: “it is
dangerous to drive”, “the discount is today”, “tomorrow it costs more”.
Urgency is the most reliable marker. Genuinely urgent chassis situations
are few: a broken ball joint, play in a wheel, brake failure. Everything
else waits calmly for a few days — long enough to get a second opinion.

## What to check yourself before the visit

Half the advantage comes from preparation. Describe the sound in writing:
when it appeared, in what conditions it is audible, what makes it worse.
Check the simple things yourself — oil level, belt condition, wheel bolt
torque. Do the coasting test: accelerate and coast in neutral. If the
sound is unchanged, the engine and gearbox are almost certainly innocent,
and an offer to rebuild the engine looks different immediately.

It helps to know in advance which family your case belongs to. A knock
that follows the road surface is the chassis, catalogued on the symptom
page
[knocking in the suspension](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-suspension/). A
knock tied to engine revs and warm-up is the engine — see
[knocking in the engine](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-engine/).

## The cheapest protection there is

Paying for an independent inspection at a workshop that will not be doing
the repair is the cheapest way to check a quoted list. A second opinion
pays for itself with the first item struck off.

Two more habits are worth building. Ask for the estimate in writing,
itemised into parts and labour: a single lump sum makes it impossible to
tell what you are agreeing to. And ask which items are safety-critical and
which are comfort — a decent mechanic separates them without hesitation,
because that is how they think about the car themselves.

Finally, it helps to arrive with the sound itself rather than a retelling
of it. Record it in the Stuk app in the situation where it is clearest.
The app matches the recording with your answers and shows the likely
causes with percentages, so the conversation starts from a specific theory
rather than a blank page.
