“It has started sounding like a diesel” is one of the most common
descriptions owners of petrol cars arrive with. What they usually mean is
a hard, chattering noise at idle, obvious with the bonnet up and audible
in the cabin with a window down. There are several possible sources, but
two lead by a wide margin: direct-injection injectors, which click by
their very nature, and valve clearances, which widen with mileage. The
difference matters: the first is usually normal, the second is scheduled
work that wears the engine faster the longer it waits.

## Injectors: a fast tick from the top and front

Direct-injection engines deliver fuel straight into the cylinder at
pressures of hundreds of atmospheres. The injector valve opens and closes
with a sharp metallic click, and together they produce the characteristic
chatter, most noticeable on a warm engine at idle.

Signs that you are hearing the injectors:

- the sound comes from the fuel rail, towards the front or the top of the
  cylinder head;
- it is even, like a sewing machine, and does not follow temperature;
- it barely changes as you raise the revs gently to 1,500–2,000 — it only
  gets faster;
- it was there from the beginning and has not grown.

The worrying variant is a tick that has become louder over recent months,
along with a misfire or a fault code on a specific cylinder. Then the
injectors are tested for flow and for leaks.

## Valve clearances: a tick at half the frequency

The valve train is driven by the camshaft, which turns at half crankshaft
speed. Hence the giveaway: clearance ticking runs at exactly half the
frequency of the firing pulses and sounds sparser than injector chatter.
It is louder on a cold engine, comes from under the valve cover, and is
heard from above.

| Sign | Injectors | Valve clearances |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency | Rapid chatter | Half as often, metronome-like |
| Source | Fuel rail | Valve cover |
| When cold | Unchanged | Often louder |
| History | Always been there | Appeared and is growing |
| Reaction to an oil change | None | Present on engines with lifters |
| Company it keeps | Cylinder fault codes, misfire | Loss of power, rough idle later on |

There is a third candidate worth remembering: knocking. It sounds like a
sharp rap on a metal tin, but appears under load at low revs and is tied
to fuel and ignition timing rather than to idle. The full portrait of
this group of sounds is on the symptom page
[ticking engine](/en/symptoms/ticking-engine/).

## The order to check

Start by recording the sound on a cold engine and on a warm one — the way
it changes separates the theories by itself. Then listen to the engine
with a stethoscope or a long screwdriver: the point of maximum noise over
the valve cover and over the fuel rail is noticeably different. Third, a
scanner: misfire codes or fuel trim corrections suggest a direction.
Fourth, measuring the clearances with feeler gauges, if the mechanism is
adjustable.

The same method — splitting the theories by the conditions the noise
appears in — works for noises outside the engine as well.

## What decides the repair

If it turns out to be injectors, there is rarely a single price: the work
depends on the injection type and the specific engine, and it starts with
testing rather than replacement — a clogged injector is often cleaned,
while a leaking one is not. If it is valve clearances, the deciding
factor is how they are adjusted: engines with screw adjusters are a short
job, while engines with shims need every clearance measured and the right
shim sizes ordered, which is why the same job differs so much between two
cars of similar size.

One thing is worth agreeing in advance in both cases: that the valve
cover gasket is replaced while the cover is off. It is a small part, and
doing it later means paying for the same disassembly twice.

Diesel-like clatter is a case where describing the sound in words is
nearly impossible and recording it is easy. Make two recordings in the
Stuk app: straight after a cold start and after ten minutes of running.
The app examines them by frequency and character and shows the likely
causes with percentages, so you arrive at the workshop with a specific
theory.
