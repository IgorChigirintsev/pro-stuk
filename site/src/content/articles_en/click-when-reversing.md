The car is parked, reverse is selected, the brake is released — and in the
first second of movement one dry click comes from below. Then silence, the
car drives normally, and nothing similar is heard going forward. The sound
is unsettling in its suddenness, but by its nature it almost always means
the same thing: somewhere in the chain from engine to wheel there is a
clearance, and when the direction of rotation changes the part shifts from
one bearing face to the other. The only question is which part, and how
big the clearance has grown.

## Why specifically in reverse

While the car drives forward, every part with play is pressed by the load
to one side: the splines bear on one flank, the pad sits at one edge of
its guide, the joint is loaded in one direction. Change direction and each
of those pairs jumps its clearance to the opposite stop. The individual
micro-jumps merge into one audible click.

So reverse works as free diagnosis: it highlights clearances that ordinary
forward driving never reveals. A click appearing does not mean a
breakdown, but it does mean the play has grown to an audible size — and
from there it only increases.

## Who clicks most often

| Source | Additional signs |
|---|---|
| Pad in the caliper | A click at the first braking after moving off, sound at the wheel |
| Outer CV joint | Crunching appears in a turn under power, grease traces |
| Inner CV joint | A thump through the body when pulling away, vibration under acceleration |
| Propshaft universal joint | Click under the centre of the floor, vibration at speed |
| Engine mount | The engine visibly nods when shifting from D to R |
| Anti-roll bar link | The click repeats over bumps, not only when starting |

Location is a convenient guide. A click at a front wheel with grease on
the arch is the driveshaft joints, covered in detail in
[signs of a worn outer CV joint](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-outer-cv-joint/)
and, for the inner one, in
[signs of a worn inner CV joint](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-inner-cv-joint/).
A click from under the centre of the floor on a rear- or four-wheel-drive
car is the propshaft, where the joint gives up first:
[signs of a worn propshaft universal joint](/en/articles/signs-of-a-worn-propshaft-universal-joint/).
The general catalogue of sounds from below is on the symptom page
[rattling under the car](/en/symptoms/rattling-under-car/), and the brake
version in

## Narrowing it down in five minutes

The checks need no lift and are done on an empty area.

1. **The repeat test.** Drive forward a metre, stop, select reverse and
   pull away. If the click repeats reliably at every change of direction,
   it is play rather than chance.
2. **The brake test.** Pull away in reverse without touching the brake,
   then brake gently. If the click appears specifically at the first press
   of the pedal, suspicion moves to the pads in the caliper.
3. **The neutral test.** Roll backwards in neutral down a slope. If the
   click has gone, the source is in the drive path; if it remains, in the
   running gear or the brakes.
4. **The steering test.** Repeat the reverse start with the steering at
   full lock. A crunch that grows is almost certainly the outer
   driveshaft joint.

## What to do about it

A single click with no vibration and no other noises on the move is a
reason to have the car looked at during the next scheduled service, not to
drop everything. A different picture calls for a prompt visit: the click
comes with a thump through the body, vibration has appeared at speed, or
crunching has started under load.

The spread in cost is wide, and the figure depends directly on how early
the source is found — an engine mount is an inexpensive part, while
letting a driveshaft joint go until the splines are damaged is not, as
covered in
[signs of worn engine mounts](/en/articles/signs-of-worn-engine-mounts/).

Two things are worth asking for at the inspection. That the mechanic
reproduces the click with the car on the ground rather than only looking
for play on a raised wheel — some clearances only show under load. And
that the brake side is ruled out first, because it is the cheapest and
most common answer.
