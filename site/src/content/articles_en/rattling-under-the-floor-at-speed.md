A rattle under the floor that appears as speed builds and fades as you
slow down almost never comes from the suspension. It comes from what is
bolted to the floor: heat shields, the exhaust system, the undertray,
plastic wheel arch liners. Those parts weigh nothing next to the car, but
each has its own resonant frequency — and when vibration from the road
and the engine lands on it, a tinny concert starts underneath. Here are
the sources, the simple checks and what happens if you leave it.

## Where a rattle at speed comes from

- **Heat shields.** Thin metal sheets between the exhaust and the floor.
  The spot welds rot away over time, the factory clamps loosen, and the
  sheet starts vibrating against the pipe. This is the single most common
  cause of rattling.
- **Exhaust hangers.** The rubber rings the system hangs from. Stretched
  hangers let the pipe wander and touch the body at certain engine
  speeds.
- **The flexible section.** The corrugated metal bellows between the
  manifold and the pipe. When its braid tears it rattles and hisses, and
  the sound grows under load.
- **The undertray and arch liners.** Loose bolts and lost clips turn a
  sheet of metal or plastic into a vibrating membrane — a clatter over
  bumps and a fine rattle at speed from the airflow.
- **A silencer breaking up inside.** Detached internal baffles rattle
  inside the box — a metallic sound that seems to roll around.

The full guide to knocks and clatter from underneath, with all their
lookalikes, is on the symptom page
[rattling under the car](/en/symptoms/rattling-under-car/).

## Speed, revs or bumps: the key question

To narrow the list, answer one question: what exactly does the rattle
follow?

| The rattle follows | How to check | Likely source |
|---|---|---|
| Engine revs | Raise the revs in neutral with the car parked | Shields, flexible section, silencer |
| Road speed | At speed, press the clutch or select neutral | Arch liners, undertray, airflow |
| Bumps | Drive over washboard tarmac slowly | Fasteners, suspension, exhaust touching the body |
| Load under acceleration | Compare gentle acceleration with a steady speed | Engine mounts, exhaust contact |

Answering that question saves money: it shortens the search from
“somewhere under the car” to a specific part before you even arrive. Give
the mechanic the conditions — speed, revs, gear — and an hour-long
inspection becomes a ten-minute one.

If the rattle can be reproduced with the car parked by raising the revs,
the source is certainly in the exhaust or its shields, and that is the
easiest case to find. If it also rings at idle, the search starts at the
front of the system. And if it is tied to the moment of acceleration and
disappears at a steady speed, see
[rattling under the car when accelerating](/en/articles/rattle-under-car-when-accelerating/) —
that one has its own mechanics: under drive the engine tilts and moves
the exhaust.

## Finding the source without a lift

The first test is stationary. With the engine warm (careful: the exhaust
is hot) ask someone to raise the revs smoothly from 800 to 3,000 while
you listen from below, from different sides. The rattle usually narrows
down to half a metre of pipe.

The second test is your hand. With the car cold, tap along the silencer
with your fist: a healthy system answers with a dull sound, a loose
shield with a tinny ring, and a broken-up silencer with a clatter inside
the box.

The third test is the fasteners. Shake the undertray, check the arch
liner clips, look at the rubber exhaust hangers: cracks and stretched
loops are visible to the eye. While you are there, see whether the pipe
hangs closer to the body on one side.

An important caveat: vibration at speed accompanied by a shaking steering
wheel is a different story, usually wheel balancing or the chassis rather
than shields. If the rattle comes with knocking over bumps and play in
the steering, the suspension deserves a separate check.

## What ignoring it leads to

A rattling shield by itself is a comfort issue, and people drive with one
for years. But every cause on the list has a sequel:

- a loose **shield** eventually breaks off; without it the heat of the
  pipe reaches the floor, cables and lines, and over dry grass that
  becomes a fire question;
- a **torn flexible section** tears further — the exhaust leak grows, the
  car gets louder, and fumes rise towards the engine bay and the cabin
  ventilation;
- a pipe sagging on stretched **hangers** wears through itself and
  whatever it hits — instead of penny rubbers you get a bill for welding;
- a loose **undertray** sheds bolts one at a time, and a sheet coming
  away at speed is dangerous both for your car and for whoever is behind.

So the sensible strategy is to find the source at the next convenient
opportunity, even if the noise does not yet bother you.

## How the repair is decided

Most rattles are closed by the cheapest line on the list: a shield
tightened or clamped, a hanger replaced — half an hour on a lift. The
questions worth asking are about the exhaust itself. If a flexible
section has torn, ask whether it is being replaced as a section or
together with the front pipe: on many cars the flex is welded in, and the
choice affects how long the repair lasts. If the silencer has broken up
inside, ask whether the system is being welded or the box replaced —
welding is cheaper but the corrosion that caused it is usually not local.
