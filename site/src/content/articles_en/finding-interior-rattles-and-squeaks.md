An interior squeak is a dry creak born where two parts of the cabin touch
each other without a spacer. It does not affect safety and does not bring
a breakdown closer, but it wears you down: the sound is there at every
joint in the tarmac, and finding its source by ear is nearly impossible —
a plastic panel reflects and spreads the squeak around the whole cabin.
The good news is that squeaks can be hunted systematically, and in most
cases the question is settled in an hour without stripping half the car.

## Where squeaks come from

A car body is not rigid: over bumps it twists slightly, and the parts
fixed inside it shift relative to one another. While there is a factory
spacer or a clearance between them, the movement passes silently. Over
time the spacer flattens, the clips lose their grip, the plastic dries out
— and dry contact appears, where one part slides against the other with a
characteristic crackle.

Hence the typical places: the join between the dashboard and the
windscreen pillars, the area around the air vents, the door cards, the
parcel shelf, the seat runners, the instrument panel, the sill trims. A
separate category is objects that are easy to forget: glasses in a
cubbyhole, coins in a cup holder, the jack under the boot floor, a child
seat not clicked fully into its mounting.

## The method: four steps

1. **Remove the extras.** Take everything out of the glovebox, the
   cubbyholes, the door pockets and the boot. If the squeak has gone,
   continue by putting the items back one at a time.
2. **Check by hand while parked.** Press firmly and twist each panel in
   turn with your palm: the dashboard by the windscreen, the pillars, the
   door cards, the headlining by the grab handles. The same timbre of
   sound under your hand means you have found the zone.
3. **Take a helper.** A passenger presses the suspect panel with a palm
   while the car drives over a rough stretch you know. The squeak stops
   under the hand — the source is right there.
4. **Separate body from interior.** Drive with one wheel along a kerb at
   low speed: that diagonal twist provokes interior squeaks more actively
   than anything else.

If after all those checks the sound remains with the doors closed and does
not react to pressure, the search should move outside — underneath the
car. The distinction is covered in detail in
[suspension creaking over bumps](/en/articles/suspension-creaking-over-bumps/)
and
[interior creaking over bumps](/en/articles/interior-creaking-over-bumps/);
the sound catalogue is on the symptom page
[knocking over bumps](/en/symptoms/knocking-over-bumps/).

## What cures what

| Zone | Typical cause | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Dashboard at the windscreen | Dry contact of plastic on glass | Felt tape in the join |
| Door card | Clips have loosened | New clips, tape on the contact |
| Instrument panel | Play in the catches | Sealing strip around the edge |
| Seat runners | Dry rails | Grease |
| Parcel shelf | Hammered-out seats | Foam on the supports |
| Belts, seat mountings | An unlatched buckle | Tighten and secure |

The main principle of the repair: a squeak is cured by separation, not by
tightening. Between two rubbing parts you need a soft material — felt,
fleece, thin closed-cell foam. Tightening a fixing harder often just moves
the squeak to the neighbouring join.

## How the decision to hand it over is made

Not every workshop takes on squeak work: it is charged by the hour and
comes with no guarantee of success first time. The criterion for doing it
yourself is simple. If there is one squeak and you roughly know where it
lives, a self-search is almost always cheaper and faster. It is worth
going to a workshop when there are several sounds, or when the suspect
zone requires the dashboard to come out — that is a job with its own risk
of new rattles afterwards.

Two things worth knowing. Before paying for interior work, have the
suspension ruled out on a lift: a knock that travels into the cabin
through the body is a completely different bill. And seasonality is its
own subject: in frost the plastic becomes stiffer and starts squeaking
even where everything was quiet in summer, which is covered in
[interior creaks in winter](/en/articles/interior-creaks-in-winter/) — a
squeak that only exists below zero rarely justifies stripping anything.

Hunting a squeak is much easier when the sound is recorded. Start
recording in the Stuk app, drive your usual rough stretch and ask a
passenger to press the panels in turn — the recording will show the moment
the squeak cut off. The app will also tell you whether the sound really is
from the interior or whether the search belongs in the suspension.
