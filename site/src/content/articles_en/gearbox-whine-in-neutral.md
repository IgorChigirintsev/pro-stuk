The car is standing still, the gearbox is in neutral, the clutch is up —
and there is an even whine from the engine bay that was not there before.
Press the clutch and the whine fades. The picture is so characteristic
that it alone identifies the part with high confidence: in the vast
majority of cases this is the input shaft bearing of a manual gearbox.
Here is why it whines exactly like that, what else sounds similar, and how
the repair is decided.

## Why a gearbox whines in neutral

In neutral with the clutch up, the gearbox input shaft is locked to the
engine through the clutch disc and turns with it. The gears spin without
load, no torque is transmitted, but the shaft bearings are working. A worn
bearing — with damaged raceways or rollers that have lost their shape —
produces an even low whine at those revs.

The moment you press the clutch, the disc separates from the flywheel and
the input shaft loses its drive and stops. There is nothing left to whine,
and the sound goes. That “up: whine, down: silence” relationship is the
main clue. The reverse picture, where the sound appears when you press the
clutch, points at the release bearing instead.

## What else sounds similar

| Source | How it behaves | The difference |
|---|---|---|
| Input shaft bearing | Whines in neutral, fades on the clutch | Follows the clutch pedal |
| Release bearing | Silent in neutral, howls on the clutch | The mirror image |
| Low gearbox oil | Whine grows when cold and in heat | Checked by the level in ten minutes |
| Alternator bearing, accessory idler | Whines regardless of pedal and gear | Audible with the belt removed |
| Water pump | Even whine, often with coolant traces | No link to the clutch |

Hence the order of checks that saves money: first the clutch pedal, then
the oil level, and only then any talk of opening the gearbox. It is also
worth listening to the car on the move: if a whine tied to a particular
speed range joins the one at a standstill, the picture changes.

## Checking it yourself

1. **The pedal test.** With the engine warm and the gearbox in neutral,
   press and release the clutch slowly several times, listening for the
   change. A clear link to the pedal is practically a diagnosis.
2. **Cold and warm.** Listen right after starting and again after twenty
   minutes of driving. A whine caused by oil usually weakens on a warm
   gearbox; mechanical wear does not.
3. **Oil level.** Switch the engine off, let the car stand on level
   ground and check the level with the dipstick or the filler plug. This
   is the cheapest possible cause.
4. **The belt test.** A workshop removes the accessory belt for a couple
   of minutes: if the whine remains, the accessories are ruled out. The
   general picture of noises on the move is on the symptom page
   [humming while driving](/en/symptoms/humming-while-driving/).

## How the repair is decided

The key fact about this job is that the gearbox has to come out. That
single point governs everything else, and it produces one clear
recommendation: if the box is being removed for a bearing, the clutch kit
is replaced at the same time. The clutch is right there, the labour is
already paid for, and doing it separately in a year means paying that
labour twice.

The same logic covers the rear main seal and the release bearing — small
parts that are only accessible with the gearbox out. A workshop that lists
them in the estimate is not padding it; one that does not mention them is
worth asking.

An even, quiet whine does not call for stopping the car: people drive with
it for months. Plan the repair when the sound becomes a rumble, vibration
appears, or metal debris shows up on the drain plug magnet — that means
the bearing is already breaking up.
