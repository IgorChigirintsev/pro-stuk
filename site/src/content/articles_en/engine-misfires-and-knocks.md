“Running on three” is what drivers used to say about a four-cylinder
engine when one cylinder stopped working. The engine shakes at idle, the
revs wander, power drops, and there can be a smell of fuel from the
tailpipe. If a knock joins in, the situation is more serious than the
average case: either the rough running is rocking worn engine mounts, or
the misfire and the knock have one mechanical cause inside the engine.
Here are both scenarios and the order to search in.

## Why a misfire and a knock often arrive together

First, the link. A dead cylinder makes crankshaft rotation uneven: the
engine jerks on every cycle, and those jerks pass into the body through
the mounts — the rubber-and-metal pads it hangs on. A worn mount answers
with dull knocks that would not be audible on a smoothly running engine.
In that scenario the knock is a consequence, and it goes away with the
misfire.

The second scenario is nastier: one mechanical fault produces both. A
burnt valve knocks and does not hold compression. Scoring in a cylinder
lowers compression and knocks with the piston skirt. A failed hydraulic
lifter ticks and stops the valve opening as it should. Here the knock is
not an echo but the voice of the fault itself.

## The causes, by group

| Group | Usual culprits | What you notice |
|---|---|---|
| Ignition | Plugs, coils, leads | Misfire under load, P030x codes |
| Fuel | An injector, fuel pressure | Misfire in every regime |
| Compression | Valve, rings, scoring | Constant misfire, knock, oil use |
| Valve train | Lifter, cam timing, chain | Ticking, misfire when cold |
| Consequences | Engine mounts | Dull knock during the jerks |

The good news: statistically most misfires come down to ignition — plugs
and coils, the cheapest line in the table. The bad news: the combination
of misfiring *and* knocking is the one that most often lands in the lower,
expensive rows.

## Finding the dead cylinder

Step one is a scan tool. It shows misfire counters per cylinder: you can
see whether one cylinder is affected or several, constantly or
occasionally. Step two is the classic swap: move the plug and coil from
the problem cylinder to a neighbouring one. If the misfire follows the
part, that part is guilty; if it stays where it was, the cause is the
injector or the mechanics of that cylinder.

If ignition and fuel are cleared, compression is measured. Low compression
in one cylinder is a reason to look inside with a borescope — a flexible
camera through the plug hole. That is how a burnt valve is told apart
from scoring on the bore walls.

## What the character of the knock tells you

The knock itself points somewhere too. Dull single impacts as you press
and release the throttle are the signature of engine mounts. An even tick
from the top of the engine is the valve train or the lifters. A sharp
metallic sound as the load changes can indicate the gudgeon pins — a rare
but distinctive case. A heavy, growing knock from the bottom of the block
combined with a misfire is the worst variant, and with that one you
should not drive under your own power at all.

It helps to compare your vibration and sound with the typical cases on the
symptom pages [vibration at idle](/en/symptoms/vibration-at-idle/) and
[knocking in the engine](/en/symptoms/knocking-in-engine/) — it makes
describing the problem to a mechanic over the phone much easier.

## Why waiting costs more than fixing

A misfiring engine damages itself and the systems around it. Unburnt fuel
from the dead cylinder washes the oil film off the bore — ring wear speeds
up and scoring appears. The same fuel finishes burning in the catalytic
converter: the ceramic melts, and instead of a set of spark plugs the
estimate now includes a converter. Finally, mounts destroyed by vibration
add their own line to the bill. That is why misfiring plus knocking is a
case where a week of delay genuinely changes the total.

One practical note about the repair: when a converter has been damaged by
a long-running misfire, replacing it without fixing the misfire first
simply destroys the new one. Any workshop that offers a converter before
the misfire is cured has the order wrong.
