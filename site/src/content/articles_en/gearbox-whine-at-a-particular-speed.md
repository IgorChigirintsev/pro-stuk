A whine that appears at, say, 70 km/h, holds for a few hundred metres and
disappears as soon as you accelerate harder sounds so odd that many
drivers write it off as the road surface. In fact that selectivity by
speed is not an accident but the signature of a mechanism: it is how gear
drives behave when the teeth or the shaft bearings are worn. Here is why
the sound is tied to a narrow window of speeds and how to tell the gearbox
from the hub and the tyres.

## Why the whine is tied to speed

Every pair of gears makes a sound at its meshing frequency as it turns —
determined by the number of teeth and the shaft speed. While the tooth
surfaces are smooth, that frequency stays a quiet background. Wear,
scoring or slack shaft bearings amplify it many times over.

Then the body comes into it. Every body has its own resonant frequencies;
when the meshing frequency coincides with one of them, the panels start
singing along and the whine becomes clearly audible in the cabin.
Accelerate and the frequency rises past the resonance, and the sound
drops away. Hence the feeling that it "only whines at 70". That is also
why the window of speeds often shifts when you change gear: the shaft
speeds change and so does the coincidence point.

## Telling the gearbox from other sources

| Source | Depends on the gear | Depends on cornering | Changes with the throttle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gearbox gears | Yes, the window shifts | No | Yes, noticeably |
| Final drive, differential | Weakly | No | Yes, different on power and coasting |
| Wheel bearing | No | Yes, grows in a bend | No |
| Tyres | No | Weakly | No, depends on the surface |
| Driveshaft bearing, halfshaft | No | Slightly | Moderately |

There are two key checks: changing gear and coasting. If at 70 km/h you
shift from fourth to third and the whine changes pitch or moves, the
source rotates inside the gearbox. If lifting off noticeably changes the
character of the sound, it depends on the load on the teeth — again the
transmission. Separating a bearing from the tyres is covered in detail in
[humming: wheel bearing or tyres](/en/articles/hum-wheel-bearing-or-tyres/),
and the general catalogue of road sounds is on the symptom page
[humming while driving](/en/symptoms/humming-while-driving/). The same
approach applied to the rear axle is in
[rear differential whine at speed](/en/articles/rear-differential-whine-at-speed/).

## What wears inside

Three things are usually to blame. The first is the shaft bearings: they
work constantly and are the first to react to overheating and old oil. The
second is the teeth of the driving and driven gears of a particular ratio;
then the whine is heard in that gear specifically, while the car is quiet
in the others. The third is the final drive and differential: their whine
is broader in range and responds more strongly to power versus coasting.

A separate and quite mundane cause is the oil. A low level, the wrong
viscosity or mileage far past the schedule worsen the lubrication, and the
gearbox starts making noise before its time. Checking the level costs
almost nothing and comes before any serious decisions. If crunching when
selecting gears joins the whine, the picture changes — that case is
covered in
[crunching when selecting a gear](/en/articles/crunching-when-selecting-a-gear/),
and a knock as the clutch comes up in

## How the repair is decided

An even whine with no vibration and no stray knocks does not call for
stopping: people drive with it for a long time, sometimes years. Take
notice if rumbling, jolting, difficult gear selection or metal particles
on the drain plug magnet appear — that is the stage at which the repair
gets more expensive with every thousand kilometres.

So the decision turns on two cheap checks before anything is opened. The
level and condition of the gearbox oil, and what is on the drain plug
magnet: a fine grey paste is normal wear, a bristle of metal shavings is
not. Together they separate "change the oil and watch it" from "plan a
rebuild".

Two things mechanics do not always say. If the gearbox has to come off,
the clutch kit is replaced at the same time — the labour is already paid
for, and reaching it again later costs the whole job. And the oil
specification matters more than the brand: gearbox oils are not
interchangeable, and the wrong friction modifier produces exactly the
noise you were trying to cure.
